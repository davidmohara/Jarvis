## What's Working

Lead with these. Rex has built something real — acknowledge it before pushing on the gaps.

- The insight is genuine. The knowledge-to-action gap is well-documented and under-solved. The framing is sharp.
- Traction is above-average for Reg CF. 94% rebooking rate, zero Fortune 500 churn, and named clients (DPR, Balfour Beatty, Advocate Health) signal real product-market fit.
- The coach/Guide flywheel is clever. External coaches pay $3,500–4,000 to certify and then sell for you. Your distribution channel funds itself.

- The capital-project model is an undervalued asset. Large construction deployments are proof that sophisticated buyers trust this product on high-stakes builds.

## Five Things to Help Him Fix

*The story works. The structure will lose sophisticated investors before they finish the document.*

**1. Complete the spin-out before the raise opens.**
"Will be airtight before close" is a deal-stopper. IP assignment must be signed, MindShift obligations clear. This is a $10K legal bill that protects his entire raise.

**2. Separate the revenue story cleanly.**
$52K GeniusSpark-specific revenue against a $3M pre-money = 58x product revenue multiple. Lead instead with 94% rebooking, zero Fortune 500 churn, $125K in contracts from insight dashboards. Position $52K as "before we productized."

**3. Build a named Year 1 pipeline.**
"Convert 10% of 180 portal clients" is a strategy, not a plan. He needs 15–20 named accounts (anonymized), deal sizes, and conversation stage. Transforms a projection into a forecast.

**4. Address the licensing risk head-on.**
If Gallup, MBTI, or Enneagram Institute decides GeniusSpark's AI prompts cross a licensing line, that's existential. He needs counsel's sign-off and a plain-English statement in the offering.

**5. Name the enterprise sales hire.**
Rex's network got them to 200 orgs. Scaling to 500–1,000 teams requires a different motion. The plan should name the VP Enterprise Sales hire, role profile, and timing.

## What to Leave Alone

- **Don't push on valuation.** $3M pre-money is defensible given parent traction, coach network, and brand. Fix the five items above and it becomes justifiable. Pushing on price reads as negotiation, not friendship.
- **Don't frame services revenue as a legacy problem.** The capital-project model (DPR, Balfour Beatty) is real money and a real differentiator in construction. It should be named and owned, not phased out.

## Key Risks & Assumptions

**Services-to-SaaS conversion is unproven.**
94% rebooking in a consulting context doesn't automatically translate to subscription conversion. These are different buyer conversations — procurement, IT security review, budget lines.

**Workflow stickiness is assumed, not demonstrated.**
Daily manager behavior change is the exact problem GeniusSpark solves for others. Whether managers actually use the two-minute prompts consistently is not disclosed.

**Assessment vendors could compete directly.**
Gallup, Myers-Briggs, and Predictive Index all have resources to build an activation layer. The product is currently too thin to defend against a determined platform move.

**Licensing exposure on third-party frameworks.**
GeniusSpark's AI-generated prompts are derived from licensed frameworks. Whether that crosses a licensing line has not been disclosed as legally cleared.

**Founder-dependent distribution.**
The certified coach network exists because of Rex's 25-year relationships. If he steps back, the network may not follow the product.

**Exit math is optimistic.**
A $45M Year 4–5 exit implies ~15x revenue on projected ARR. Predictive Index is named as a likely acquirer — but PI is PE-backed and acquisition appetite is assumed, not confirmed.
