# Meeting Coaching Review — Coffee with Steve Hall (2026-07-14)

**This version supersedes the earlier one from today.** That review was built on a vault note explicitly labeled as curated excerpts, about 15 of the meeting's 46 minutes. This one is built on the complete transcript, and it overturns several of the earlier conclusions. Where it does, I say so directly rather than quietly dropping the old point.

## Where You Showed Up Well

- **Fast, credible pattern match, confirmed.** The instant Steve described the Rogue pricing problem, you reached for the pharmaceutical raw-goods arbitrage case (11:32) instead of improvising. Recognizing the shape of a problem you'd already solved and naming it immediately is the right instinct for an active seller.
- **You reframed a hard problem into a simpler one, live.** This is new information that wasn't in the excerpt. At 20:12, mid-conversation, you stopped and said: "what you described to me isn't a workflow problem; it's a modeling problem, which is just a training issue, which is an even simpler solve." That's a real-time diagnostic reframe that changes the shape of the deal, smaller, faster, cheaper to deliver, and you did it out loud, not in a follow-up email. That's the single strongest moment in the meeting.
- **You proactively surfaced the ownership gap, twice, before Steve forced the issue.** At 14:12 you asked "where does that live from a technology perspective?" This came before Steve's "here is the data, live or no" (14:21). The earlier review had this backwards, crediting you only with answering Steve's question well. You actually raised the hard question first.
- **You offered a real interim solution to the staffing gap, unprompted.** At 16:17 you built the "boarding the dogs" analogy live: Improving can carry the workflow short term, the way a boarding service handles a dog while the owner travels, but it's explicitly not the long-term answer. That's you solving half of Steve's problem for him in the room, not just flagging it.
- **You demoed proof of your own product, live, at the perfect moment.** At 25:26 to 27:41, you walked Steve through your own scheduled-task news dashboard, unprompted, as a direct illustration of exactly the kind of automation you'd just proposed for his pricing workflow. Showing a working example of your own tooling mid-pitch is a strong credibility move that wasn't visible in the excerpt at all.
- **You stayed engaged through a long relationship-building open, not passive.** The first nine minutes cover World Cup, Cabo, and the Just Capital pitch. You weren't just listening. You contributed a specific, researched data point on AI and job growth (06:56 to 08:51) that showed you'd already been thinking about the exact narrative Steve was building toward. That's active participation in rapport-building, correctly read as such.

## Where You Could Have Better Controlled the Conversation

- **The Just Capital ask, corrected.** At 09:11, Steve said "So Chris is like, I want to get Devlin, David and me involved." Correction from the earlier version of this review: that's Steve relaying what Curtis Hite wants, not a live ask Steve himself was making to you across the table. There's no interpersonal dynamic to score there, no boundary for you to set against Steve in that moment, because Steve wasn't the one asking. What it does leave open is a secondhand commitment (Curtis wants three specific people in as founding members) that hasn't been confirmed directly with Curtis. Worth closing that loop with Curtis, not something to fault your in-room conversational control for.
- **You let the internal-ownership conversation stay abstract a beat too long.** At 15:20, Steve asked "how technical is that?" sizing a role against a real person. You gave a solid general answer (technical-ish, needs to know if it's connectivity or an error). Steve then supplied his own candidate at 15:44 ("we have an IT person that needs something"). You could have gotten there faster by asking directly, "who's the most IT-adjacent person on your team right now?" rather than waiting for Steve to volunteer it.
- **One structural point from the earlier review still holds, restated correctly this time.** Both automotive use cases, CRM-driven lead workflow and inventory pricing, landed close together (09:31 to roughly 20:12) before you fully separated them. You did eventually split them cleanly ("let me bifurcate what you are talking about," 11:32, and again with the modeling reframe at 20:12), so the separation happened, just later than it could have. Doing it at first mention would have saved Steve some of the back-and-forth before the reframe landed.

## Openings You Gave the Other Person

- The direct "No" at 14:23, in response to "is this live," pulled Steve straight into naming the three systems involved (14:51) without you having to ask. Short, honest answers created room there.
- Asking "where does that live from a technology perspective?" (14:12) opened the door Steve walked through at 15:44 with the IT-person candidate. That's a good example of a question doing the work instead of you supplying the answer yourself.
- Less effective: the pharma-analogy explanation (11:32) and the use-case-method explanation (12:25) ran back to back with no check-in question between them. Worth watching for, since fully completing a thought before pausing for the other person is a comfortable default and it's easy to keep doing it once it's working.

## Openings You Missed From the Room

- **32:34, Steve:** "What's the best way to start the process?" You answered well (get us the person handling the data, we can scope from there), but Steve had just asked a buying question directly. A sharper version locks a specific date in that exact breath rather than two exchanges later. As it happened, Steve himself proposed the next step at 33:12 ("let's set up a time... we'll spend a day with Bud"), which is a good outcome, but it means Steve closed the meeting, not you. Worth noting for next time: when a prospect asks "what's the best way to start," that's the moment to hand back a specific date, not a process description.

## Overall Presence

Composed, credible, and technically sharp throughout, and more directive than the excerpt-based read gave you credit for. You didn't just answer Steve's questions in sequence; you asked the harder question first (14:12), reframed his problem into a smaller one live (20:12), solved half his staffing problem out loud (16:17), and proved your own methodology with a live demo (25:26). That is active, not reactive.

The Just Capital commitment is now correctly understood as secondhand (Curtis's ask, relayed by Steve), so it's not a live in-room control gap. With that removed, the meeting reads as more consistently directive than either prior version credited: you asked the hard question first (14:12), reframed a problem live (20:12), solved half a staffing problem out loud (16:17), and demoed your own methodology (25:26). The one open item left isn't conversational control, it's a follow-up: confirm the founding-member ask directly with Curtis, since that's the actual party who made it.
