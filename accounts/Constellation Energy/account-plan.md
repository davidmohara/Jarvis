## 1. Read this first: the entity and the opening

The target is **Constellation Energy Corporation (Nasdaq: CEG)**, headquartered in Baltimore, the largest private-sector power producer in the world and the largest producer of carbon-free energy in the U.S. The "Houston" connection is real and it matters more now than when the training bid happened. Two things anchor Houston:

1. Constellation's **commercial and industrial (C&I) retail business** runs a major office at 1001 Louisiana St, Houston. This is the old Constellation NewEnergy operation and the most likely home of the AI training opportunity you lost.
2. In **January 2026 Constellation closed its acquisition of Calpine**, the Houston-based independent power producer. Total consideration transferred was **$21.8B** against a deal enterprise value of roughly **$26.6B**. Constellation describes the combined platform as **55 GW** of capacity and explicitly commits to "a significant presence in Houston." Integrating Calpine's people and plants is a stated top corporate priority, and it is centered in Houston.

**[Updated 2026-10-23]** The integration is now late-stage rather than early. Constellation has completed the DOJ-required divestitures and told the market in its Q2 2026 10-Q that design, restructuring and integration activities were still ongoing as of June 30, 2026. The window for "surge capacity during an integration" is narrowing rather than closed, which changes urgency but not the thesis.

The loss reframes cleanly: a single training SOW was a transactional entry. The real account is an energy company with $22.7B of FY2025 revenue before Calpine, a materially larger pro forma run rate, and a public bet on powering the AI data center boom. That is a digital, data, and modernization story, not a training story.

**[CRM re-pull 2026-10-23, via Dataverse Web API]** The account now has opportunity history where July found none: **Constellation - AI Governance, $310.4K est. value, created 2026-02-23, closed Lost 2026-04-24**, and **Constellation - Customer Mobile App Development, $240K, closed Lost 2024-11-22**. ## 2. What Constellation has told the market it cares about

**[Refreshed 2026-10-23]** Sourced from the FY2025 10-K (filed Feb 2026), the Q1 and Q2 2026 10-Qs, Constellation's 2025-2026 press releases, and the Q2 2026 earnings release. The company's stated priorities have not reversed since July. They have sharpened, and one genuinely new technology priority appeared in October.

### Priority 1: Powering the AI and data center boom, now executed through bilateral deals rather than co-location tariffs

The FY2025 10-K names "New Technologies Requiring Reliable Energy" and names Microsoft, Google and Amazon AI investments as demand drivers. Since July the deal cadence has accelerated and the structure has clarified: Constellation is signing long-term PPAs directly with hyperscalers, not waiting on FERC co-location rules.

- **Google (announced Oct 6, 2026).** A 20-year PPA funding **890 MW of new nuclear uprates across 11 units** at six sites in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, plus a separate 15-year agreement for **2,700 MW** of PJM energy supply. Constellation frames it as **$4.3B of new investment**, sustaining roughly 4,400 existing jobs and creating roughly 7,200 construction jobs. First uprate expected by 2028.
- **Amazon (announced Sept 30, 2026).** A 20-year agreement at **Calvert Cliffs** covering **690 MW**, including a **190 MW uprate** coming online between 2030 and 2032, underpinning more than **$3B** of Maryland infrastructure investment, plus a related retail supply agreement across PJM.
- **Walmart (June 2026, 176 MW)** at Dresden in Illinois, enabling a 30 MW capacity expansion.
- **920 MW** of additional long-term nuclear PPAs signed in Q2 2026 with investment-grade customers, 15 to 20 years, starting 2029 through 2032.
- **CyrusOne** is the Texas co-location flagship: a 380 MW agreement plus an exclusive Phase 2 at another 380 MW at Freestone Energy Center, on top of 400 MW across two agreements at Thad Hill Energy Center.

**Microsoft Three Mile Island (Crane) and the Meta Clinton PPA remain the earlier flagship deals.** Crane slipped slightly: the Q2 release points to restart in **2027** rather than 2028, after FERC granted a Capacity Interconnection Rights waiver and the NRC approved the fuel license amendment.

### Priority 2: Data economy is now a named business with its own executive

This is the most important open item the plan closed. The plan had recorded a newly created "SVP, Finance and Data Economy" role with the name unknown. That was incomplete: Constellation announced the post in November 2025 as a **Senior Executive Vice President, Finance and Data Economy**, reporting to Dominguez, and named **Daniel L. Eggers** to it. Eggers was CFO from the company's 2022 spin and was executive sponsor of both the Crane restart and the Calpine integration. Shane Smith took the CFO seat. Eggers' public bio states he "leads Constellation's data economy business" and is "driving the development of innovative solutions to support customer needs and unlock new value streams." Read plainly: Constellation is trying to build and sell data-derived products to its own 2.5 million customer accounts, not just generate and retail electrons. That it is an SEVP post reporting to the CEO, not a VP post buried in IT, is the resourcing signal.

### Priority 3: AI is moving into the operating core, with Google as the named platform partner

The October 6 announcement carries a second, separate component that matters more to Improving than the megawatts: an **expanded five-year technology alliance** under which Constellation "selected Google Cloud and Gemini Enterprise" to establish an "AI for energy" blueprint, with Gemini Enterprise agentic workflows built "directly into its core operations." Three named focus areas:

- **Speed to power:** AI for site selection, power flow modeling, permitting and interconnection planning.
- **Generation optimization:** asset health monitoring, outage and turnaround management, scenario optimization.
- **Protecting critical infrastructure:** Google Cloud defense-grade platforms securing operational technology networks, many of them air-gapped and connected to the bulk electric system.

This is the first primary-source confirmation of Constellation's AI platform direction, and it is the most important paragraph in this document for partner strategy. See Section 3b.

### Priority 4: The Calpine integration is late-stage but not finished

The Q2 2026 10-Q states that design, restructuring and integration activities were still ongoing as of June 30, 2026, and that **$11.1B of goodwill is only provisionally allocated** to the Calpine segment, subject to revision "as future organizational, restructuring, and integration activities are completed." DOJ-mandated divestitures are closing: Brazos Valley (606 MW, ERCOT) sold to LS Power for $860M closed Oct 14, 2026, and roughly 4.4 GW of PJM gas assets (Bethlehem, York 1, York 2, Hay Road, Edge Moor) are expected to close by year-end 2026. Calpine merger and integration costs are still running through adjusted earnings. Systems and data consolidation work outlives the org chart, so this remains the largest funded program on the board even as the headline integration winds down.

### Priority 5: Commercial and C&I differentiation, now including offsite renewables

The FY2025 10-K describes expanding "digital applications providing tools, tracking, and measurement" and the **Constellation Navigator** platform, which pairs software with advisors to help C&I customers baseline emissions and hit sustainability targets. The retail book is about **2.5 million customer accounts** including **80% of the Fortune 100**. Product expansion continues: **Constellation Offsite Renewable (CORe)** now fronts third-party projects, for example the 15-year Toyota agreement (Sept 21, 2026), backed by a 115 MW PPA with Ferrovial's Milano Solar project in Milam County, Texas. C&I direct market share was reported above 32% with renewal rates of 78% (power) and 88% (gas). A large, software-differentiated book of business run substantially out of Houston.

**Also stated, unchanged:** workforce skills. The 10-K lists "training, reskilling, and upskilling" as one of three workforce pillars and lists "ability to attract and retain an appropriately qualified workforce" as a risk factor. The original training thesis was correct; it was sold as a standalone instead of as part of a transformation.

**Market context, not a stated priority (flagged as inference):** on **Oct 12, 2026 FERC rejected most of PJM's large-load interconnection and curtailment plan** while approving PJM's data center registry. That removes the tariff path for co-located load and makes bilateral "bring your own power" structures like the Google and Amazon deals the practical route to market. Constellation is positioned as the counterparty of choice in exactly that structure, which is a tailwind for the commercial org and for the data products the data economy group would sell alongside it.
