## 1. Read this first: the entity and the opening

The target is **Constellation Energy Corporation (Nasdaq: CEG)**, headquartered in Baltimore, the largest producer of carbon-free energy in the U.S. The "Houston" connection is real and it matters more now than when the training bid happened. Two things anchor Houston:

1. Constellation's **commercial and industrial (C&I) retail business** runs a major office at 1001 Louisiana St, Houston. This is the old Constellation NewEnergy operation and the most likely home of the AI training opportunity you lost.
2. In **January 2026 Constellation closed its ~$22B acquisition of Calpine**, the Houston-based independent power producer. Integrating Calpine's ~2,300 employees and 60+ plants is now a stated top corporate priority. This is the single biggest services catalyst on the board and it is centered in Houston.

The loss reframes cleanly: a single training SOW was a transactional entry. The real account is a $30B-revenue energy company in the middle of the largest integration in its history, publicly betting the company on powering the AI data center boom. That is a digital, data, and modernization story, not a training story.

## 2. What Constellation has told the market it cares about

Sourced from the FY2024 10-K (filed Feb 2025), the Q1 2026 10-Q, and 2025-2026 announcements. These are *their stated* priorities, which is what we align to.

**Powering the AI / data center boom is the core thesis.** The 10-K explicitly calls out "New Technologies Requiring Reliable Energy" and names Microsoft, Google, and Amazon AI investments as drivers of "unprecedented demand." Strategy includes **co-location of data centers** adjacent to plants. The Microsoft Three Mile Island restart (~835 MW, online ~2028) and the Meta Clinton PPA are the flagship deals. Expect a run of co-location announcements through 2026.

**Calpine integration is the near-term operational priority.** Total consideration ~$21.8B, ~$18.5B of added PP&E, ~$11.1B goodwill, ~60 GW combined fleet. The stated short-term focus is "seamless integration" of 2,300 employees and 60+ plants. Integrations of this size are где systems, data, and process consolidation work lives: ERP/asset-management harmonization, data migration and reconciliation, identity and access, application rationalization, reporting consolidation.

**The C&I commercial business is competing on digital tools, not just commodity.** The 10-K describes expanding "digital applications providing tools, tracking, and measurement" and the launch of **Constellation Navigator**, an "advanced technology platform" pairing software with advisors to help C&I customers baseline emissions and hit sustainability targets. C&I direct market share is >32% with renewal rates of 78% (power) / 88% (gas) — a large, software-differentiated book of business run substantially out of Houston.

**Workforce skills are a named strategic focus and a named risk.** The 10-K lists "training, reskilling, and upskilling" as one of three workforce pillars, and lists "ability to attract and retain an appropriately qualified workforce" as a risk factor. Your original training thesis was *correct* — it was just sold as a standalone instead of as part of a transformation.

**Technology leadership sits under the Chief Administration Officer.** Mike Koehler (EVP & CAO) owns IT, strategic technology platforms, cybersecurity, and digital strategy — a former Chief Information & Chief Digital Officer. Technology decisions concentrate here, not in a standalone CIO org.

Map each to a stated Constellation need. Confirm the specific win-wire references with your delivery and marketing teams before naming clients externally.

**3. Capability building that sticks because it's tied to delivery.** This is the reframe of the lost deal and a genuine Improving differentiator: you don't sell classroom training, you build capability *inside real delivery* (coaching, pairing, technical enablement on the actual codebase). This directly serves their stated "training, reskilling, upskilling" pillar and the "attract and retain qualified workforce" risk — but as transformation, not a course. *Win-wire to pull:* an engagement where Improving teams upskilled a client's staff while shipping.

**Honest competitive note:** Constellation is a large enterprise that almost certainly has incumbent Big-4/SI relationships (Accenture, Deloitte, etc.) on major programs. Improving does not win on scale or brand against those firms. We win on *senior talent density, delivery speed, capability transfer, and lower-ego partnership* — being the team that actually ships and leaves the client stronger. Position into the gaps the big SIs leave: the overloaded integration workstream, the data foundation nobody owns, the team that needs to level up while delivering. Compete on outcomes per dollar, not headcount.
