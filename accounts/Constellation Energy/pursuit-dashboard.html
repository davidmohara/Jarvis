AI Foundation Assessment

A short, paid discovery engagement: where Constellation's data foundation stands relative to its public AI ambitions (co-location siting, demand response, agentic tooling), with a prioritized roadmap. Lowest commitment of the three plays, and naturally expands into engineering work. Sponsor: Shaina Green, VP of Digital.
“Your external thesis is that Constellation is the clean-power backbone for the AI boom. Internally, the constraint on most digital orgs I work with isn't strategy — it's the engineering capacity to turn AI intent into production systems on a data foundation that's solid. With Calpine just folded in, I'd guess that foundation question got harder overnight. Where's that friction showing up for your team?”
Why lead here: Green is the one contact who can sponsor any of the three plays, and a low-commitment paid discovery reframes Improving as a strategic partner rather than the vendor that lost a training bid. Use it to earn a referral to Daniel Horvath for the bigger integration-surge play (Option A).

“You've built >32% direct C&I share and you're differentiating Navigator on technology, not commodity. As that roadmap accelerates, where are you feeling the constraint today — feature velocity, data integration, or getting your teams fluent enough to ship AI-assisted features?”

Strategic Priorities (Constellation, 2026)
  • Powering the AI/data center boom: the 10-K names Microsoft, Google, and Amazon AI investments as demand drivers; co-location of data centers adjacent to plants; flagship deals include the Microsoft Three Mile Island restart and the Meta Clinton PPA.
  • Calpine integration: ~$21.8B consideration, ~60GW combined fleet, ~2,300 employees. Stated near-term focus is "seamless integration" — ERP/asset-management harmonization, data migration/reconciliation, identity/access, application rationalization, reporting consolidation.
  • C&I digital differentiation: Constellation Navigator pairs software with advisors for emissions/sustainability tracking. >32% direct C&I share, 78%/88% renewal rates — a large, software-differentiated book run substantially out of Houston.
  • Workforce skills: "training, reskilling, and upskilling" is a named strategic pillar; "ability to attract and retain an appropriately qualified workforce" is a named risk factor.
  • Technology leadership under the CAO: Mike Koehler (EVP & CAO) owns IT, strategic technology platforms, cybersecurity, and digital strategy — concentrated, not a standalone CIO org.
Competitive Positioning
  • AI + data engineering as a paired competency: Constellation just created an SVP "Finance & Data Economy" role — data is now a P&L lever. Improving pairs data engineering (foundation) with applied AI (value) rather than selling models on broken data.
  • Modernization-at-pace for a live M&A integration: the Calpine integration is the highest-urgency, best-funded program in the company. Improving's embedded, outcome-based delivery teams augment an overloaded integration office.
  • Capability building tied to delivery: directly reframes the lost training deal — not classroom training, but capability built inside real delivery (coaching, pairing, technical enablement on the actual codebase).

Honest competitive note: Constellation almost certainly has incumbent Big-4/SI relationships (Accenture, Deloitte, etc.) on major programs. Improving doesn't win on scale or brand — it wins on senior talent density, delivery speed, capability transfer, and lower-ego partnership. Compete into the gaps the big SIs leave: the overloaded integration workstream, the unowned data foundation, the team that needs to level up while delivering.
