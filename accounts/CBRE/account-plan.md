### 3. M&A Integration Discipline — A Real Organizational Constraint
M&A remains CBRE's top capital-allocation priority, but with a disciplined approach: tuck-in acquisitions in the $50–300M enterprise value range with IRR hurdles exceeding 15%. **Integration discipline is an active organizational constraint**, not a solved problem. CBRE's integration philosophy: acquisitions work only if (1) they add unique talent or capability, and (2) integration stays tight with local teams maintaining high service levels. Each acquisition (Pearce at $1.2B, Industrious in flexible workplace, earlier Direct Line Global) requires significant systems/process integration work without losing local teams or service quality. This is flagged explicitly by leadership as a known execution risk and bandwidth constraint.

**October 2026 update: the deal size band has moved up, and the integration load has increased again.** Onnec, at up to roughly $1B including earnout, sits well above the $50–300M tuck-in range this plan previously described. On the Q3 call, management confirmed the deal priced at roughly 9x EBITDA on an upfront basis, with the multiple rising if the earnout is fully achieved. CFO Emma Giamartino: *"if they earn the entirety of the earnout, that would be a little over $1 billion."* Management restated the criteria as immediately accretive with a target return of at least 15%, and confirmed *"a strong pipeline, particularly in the data center space"* with more M&A expected in 2027. Buybacks were slowed in Q3 ahead of Onnec and will slow further in Q4. The live integration load is now Pearce (closed November 2025), Onnec (closing around year-end 2026, subject to regulatory approval), the Turner & Townsend 30% minority interest, and whatever closes in 2027.

**What this means:** CBRE is acquiring faster than it can comfortably integrate. The portfolio expansion strategy outpaces the organization's bandwidth to absorb acquisitions while managing organic growth in higher-margin niches. This is a specific, named constraint in the earnings calls and investor presentations.

## Entry Plays / Opportunity Scenarios

*Rebuilt this revision to lead with CBRE's own stated business constraints and execution priorities, not any partner-referral relationship. No entry play below depends on an existing "open door" — each has to be earned through a grounded, credible conversation addressing a specific, named constraint.*

### Option A — Critical Infrastructure & Data Center Delivery Capacity *(recommended lead)*
CBRE's own numbers and leadership statements make this the strongest, most current hook, and the October 2026 refresh made it stronger. Critical infrastructure services revenue reached **$744M in Q3 2026, up 80% from $414M a year earlier**, driven by hyperscaler demand for data center services plus Pearce Services. Company-wide infrastructure services revenue hit **$4.5B trailing twelve months, up 50%**. CBRE is integrating the **$1.2B Pearce Services** acquisition while closing **Onnec** (up to ~$1B including earnout) around year-end, and has committed publicly to **nearly $4.5B of Critical Infrastructure Services revenue and nearly $7.0B of total infrastructure services revenue in 2027**. Leadership states plainly that **"delivery becomes the constraint."** The LevelUp partnership with Meta exists to manufacture technician supply, which tells you how tight delivery capacity is.

The gap is not market demand or strategy. It is delivery bandwidth, and after Onnec it is also integration bandwidth across two acquisitions in twelve months plus a 30% minority partner at Turner & Townsend. CBRE needs data and systems integration support for newly acquired businesses, real-time data integration to support infrastructure operations at scale, and velocity to move on new projects without losing quality.

**Grounding (offering pull 2026-10-23):** the commercial shape of this play is a **Data Enablement Engagement** (Data Enablement category; Duration 4-8 weeks; Price $125,000-$250,000; source: `Sales Offerings/Specific Offerings/Data Enablement/Data Enablement Engagement.docx`), scoped to one integration surface left behind by the Pearce/Onnec integration. Sustained pod capacity beyond the first engagement maps to **Embedded Delivery Capacity (Microsoft Stack)**, which exists today only as a **draft GMS offering outline** (2026-09-18, 12-26 weeks, land size $150K-$400K placeholder, no named offering owner) and must be validated with the Chief Consulting Officer's office before it is quoted. Sell the scoped, published engagement first; do not sell the draft pod model as if it were a product.

**Entry pitch:** "You just committed to nearly $7 billion of infrastructure services revenue in 2027, up more than 40%, and you're closing Onnec on top of Pearce. Leadership says delivery is the constraint, not demand. We already have an agreement in place with CBRE. Pick one integration surface from the Pearce or Onnec work and give us four to eight weeks on it, a scoped data and systems integration engagement with a fixed shape and a fixed price, and judge us on whether it lands before the 2027 number gets locked in."

**Why this got stronger, not weaker.** The Q3 call added a second, harder-edged opening that did not exist in July: CBRE disclosed **"a couple of accounts that are problematic that we've decided to take on and work out,"** where the contracted scope *"just doesn't work the way it's structured now."* That is a named, dated, owner-assigned delivery problem inside Enterprise Facilities Management, with Q4 pressure attached. It is the most concrete piece of pain CBRE has said out loud in this entire research trail, and it is remediation work rather than transformation work, which is squarely Improving's shape.

### Option B — M&A Integration Capacity & Systems Consolidation
CBRE is acquiring faster than it can comfortably integrate: **Pearce ($1.2B, closed November 2025)**, **Onnec (up to ~$1B, announced October 20, 2026, closing around year-end)**, Direct Line Global (June 2024), and Industrious in flexible workplace. M&A was confirmed again this quarter as the top capital-allocation priority, with *"a strong pipeline, particularly in the data center space"* and more deals expected to convert in 2027. **Integration discipline is an active organizational constraint.**

**One detail makes this sharper than a generic integration pitch.** Onnec's consideration is **£460M upfront plus up to £300M (about $400M) tied to multi-year growth thresholds**. CBRE has roughly $400M of purchase price contingent on Onnec growing quickly after close, and CFO Emma Giamartino said *"we're very confident that they will."* Integration quality is not a soft benefit here. It carries a price tag, and anything that accelerates Onnec's systems and data integration after close directly protects that earnout.

**Grounding (offering pull 2026-10-23):** partially grounded. Improving has **no dedicated M&A-integration offering** in the current Sales Offerings tree. The closest real shapes are the **Application Modernization** engagement (Application Modernization category; assess the current state of the client's applications and deliver a modernization roadmap; the internal delivery guide states no Duration/Price; source: `Sales Offerings/Specific Offerings/Application Modernization/Application Modernization - Internal Delivery Guide.docx`) applied to a newly acquired unit's application estate, plus the **draft Embedded Delivery Capacity outline** for sustained pod work (CCO validation required before quoting). The earnout-acceleration framing is real and stays; the "we've done this at comparable scale" line was removed because no clearable M&A-integration reference was confirmed in this pass.

**Entry pitch:** "You've got Pearce closed, Onnec closing around year-end, and a pipeline you expect to convert in 2027. Onnec's earnout is tied to growth thresholds, so integration speed is worth real money to you. Give us one acquired unit's application estate: a fixed-scope assessment that inventories the systems, maps the dependencies, and hands you a modernization and consolidation roadmap you can execute with or without us. Then judge whether the playbook earns the next one."

### Option C — AI Platform Engineering Velocity (Ellis AI / Vantage Analytics Extension)
Extend Ellis AI or Vantage Analytics (2026 CIO 100 Award winner for its Next Action Engine) into new use cases via embedded AI engineering capacity. The platforms are proven and award-winning; the constraint is engineering velocity to extend them into new domains, integrate them with operations/infrastructure systems, and roll out globally without losing data governance.

**Grounding (offering pull 2026-10-23):** the AI offerings underneath this play are real and published: the **AI / ML Discovery Workshop & Consulting Engagement** (AI Adoption Transformation; Duration 2 weeks; Price $25,000 per engagement; source: `Sales Offerings/Specific Offerings/AI Offerings/AI Adoption Transformation/Artificial Intelligence Offering.docx`) and the **AI Team Enablement Deep Learning Program** (AI Software Engineering - Transformation; Duration 12 weeks per track, six half-day workshops plus embedded AI engineers; priced per track, custom quote; source: `.../AI Offerings/AI Software Engineering - Transformation/AI Team Transformation.docx`). So this play is offering-grounded but competitively demoted: the offerings exist, the lane is owned by McKinsey and Google Cloud.

**Entry pitch:** "Your AI platforms are award-winners, and we are not here to compete with what McKinsey and Google Cloud are building with you. Where we can help is the engineering layer underneath: the data and systems integration work that decides whether Ellis and Vantage ever reach the operational systems they are supposed to change. That is the work we do."

**Recommendation: Lead with Option A, and treat Option C as contested territory.** Option A is grounded in CBRE's own October 2026 numbers and 2027 commitments, leverages Improving's existing PSA (procurement friction already solved), and is now also offering-grounded: it maps to the published Data Enablement Engagement (4-8 weeks, $125K-$250K), a scoped shape CBRE can say yes to without a platform decision. Option B is the natural second conversation, and the Onnec earnout gives it a hard commercial hook; it is partially grounded (Application Modernization assessment shape, no dedicated M&A offering), so scope it as the assessment, not as an invented integration service. **Option C stays demoted.** Its offerings are real, but CBRE's own materials show McKinsey and Google Cloud running the agentic AI platform build, so Improving would be arriving third into a funded program with named incumbents. Enter it later, through an infrastructure or integration proof point, and as supplementary engineering capacity rather than as a platform alternative.
