## Texas Instruments' Stated Strategic Priorities

Sourced from the FY2025 10-K (filed February 6, 2026), the February 4, 2026 Silicon Labs acquisition announcement, the Q1, Q2, and Q3 2026 earnings call transcripts, the Q3 2026 Form 10-Q (filed October 23, 2026), and TI's own capital-management investor materials. Five themes. Themes 1 through 4 are carried forward with October 2026 updates; Theme 5 is new in this refresh.

### 1. Edge AI and Embedded Connectivity, Anchored by the Silicon Labs Acquisition
TI's $7.5B all-cash acquisition of Silicon Labs (announced Feb 4, 2026, enterprise value ~$7.5B) is explicitly positioned to "create a global leader in embedded wireless connectivity solutions," combining Silicon Labs' mixed-signal/connectivity IP with TI's analog and embedded-processing portfolio as the IoT ecosystem "pivots toward edge AI computing." Silicon Labs' Series 3 platform is cited by name as delivering a 10x AI-performance uplift (100 AI GOPS vs. ~10 GOPS/300 in the prior generation). TI also frames the deal as leveraging its own internally-owned, low-cost 300mm manufacturing capacity (Sherman, Texas) to scale Silicon Labs' ~1,200-product wireless connectivity portfolio.

**Refresh, October 2026.** The deal has moved from announcement into regulatory execution. The HSR waiting period expired May 22, 2026, Singapore's competition authority cleared the transaction August 7, 2026, and TI still guides to a close in the first half of 2027, funded from cash on hand plus a $5 billion delayed draw term loan arranged in June 2026 and undrawn at September 30, 2026. On the Q3 2026 earnings call, management described Silicon Labs as providing "another clean room" and supporting migration of foundry-based embedded products into TI's Lehi, Utah fab, which is expected to help embedded gross margins (up about 10 points year over year). Acquisition charges were $41 million in the first nine months of 2026.

### 2. Completing and Ramping the $60B+ U.S. Manufacturing Buildout (Sherman, TX and Lehi, UT)
TI has invested more than $60B across seven 300mm wafer fabs in Texas (Sherman) and Utah (Lehi) — the largest investment in foundational semiconductor manufacturing in U.S. history, supporting as many as 3,000 direct jobs at the Sherman site alone. TI's FY2025 10-K and capital-management materials describe the current phase as ramping production and qualification at these newest fabs, with capital expenditure now declining as the six-year build-out cycle ends (2026 capex narrowed on the Q3 2026 call to $2B to $3B, aiming at the higher end, down from the peak investment years), explicitly to support higher free cash flow.

**Refresh, October 2026.** Capex for the first nine months of 2026 was $1.653B versus $3.625B a year earlier, and Q3 capex alone was $463M. CHIPS Act incentive proceeds were $1.104B in the first nine months of 2026 versus $335M a year earlier, for a total cash benefit of $1.484B versus $581M. The buildout is not finished, though: in July 2026 TI received a roughly $33.6M Texas state grant for expansion of its Richardson, Texas facility, and on the Q3 2026 call management said it is ramping its Malaysia back-end assembly and test site and seeing extending lead times on back-end tools, which it characterized as an execution constraint rather than a structural gate. 2027 capex was not quantified; TI said it will modulate the incoming rate against revenue growth, with more detail in February 2027.
Sources: [TI reports Q4 2025 and 2025 financial results and shareholder returns](https://investor.ti.com/news-releases/news-release-details/ti-reports-q4-2025-and-2025-financial-results-and-shareholder); [Texas Instruments Capital Management Review — Investor Relations](https://investor.ti.com/static-files/1890477c-9df1-4e66-b43a-80521e40e4e1); [Sherman, Texas: 300mm wafer fabs — TI.com](https://www.ti.com/about-ti/company/ti-at-a-glance/manufacturing/sherman.html); [Inside Texas Instruments' New 300mm Fab in Sherman, Texas — EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/inside-texas-instruments-new-300mm-fab-in-sherman-texas/); [TI Form 10-Q for the quarter ended September 30, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/97476/000009747626000169/txn-20260930.htm); Dallas News, July 9, 2026 (Richardson state grant); Texas Instruments Q3 2026 earnings call, Oct 21, 2026

### 3. Capital Efficiency and Free Cash Flow Per Share as the Primary Financial Target
TI's stated long-term strategic focus is growth of free cash flow (FCF) per share as "the ultimate measure to generate value," targeting a 10% CAGR with a "significant step forward" expected in 2026 as CapEx declines from its investment-cycle peak. TI increased its dividend 4% to $1.42/share and repurchased $1.5B of shares in 2025, reaffirming a commitment to "return all free cash flow to owners over time." The August 1, 2026 CFO transition (Julie Knecht succeeding the retiring Rafael Lizardi) is explicitly framed around continuity on this exact priority — CEO Haviv Ilan credited Lizardi's "focus on disciplined capital allocation" and said Knecht shares that commitment.

**Refresh, October 2026.** In September 2026 TI raised the dividend another 7% to $1.52 per share, its 23rd consecutive annual increase, payable November 10, 2026. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow was $8.4B, or 40.6% of revenue, including $1.6B of CHIPS Act incentives; TI returned $5.8B to shareholders over that period. Share repurchases slowed to $233M in the first nine months of 2026, versus $1.074B a year earlier, as TI preserves capacity for the Silicon Labs deal. Management reiterated on the Q3 2026 call the commitment to return all free cash flow to owners over time, and 2027's effective tax rate is guided to 13% to 14%. Knecht led the call as CFO, which confirms the transition executed on August 1, 2026.

### 4. Internalizing Embedded Production and Operational Efficiency
TI's own stated strategy calls out rapid internalization of embedded-processing production, targeting over 90% in-house manufacturing by the end of the decade, paired with continued operational-efficiency gains as new 300mm capacity comes online. TI's Q2 2026 earnings call reported industrial demand up 30% YoY, automotive turning up mid-teens YoY, and data-center demand doubling YoY — end-market signals reinforcing the case for scaled, efficient internal production.

**Refresh, October 2026.** Q3 2026 extended the trend: industrial grew more than 40% YoY to a record level above its prior 2022 peak, automotive grew mid-teens with hybrid EV and powertrain growing at roughly twice the segment rate, and the company reported no customer inventory buildup. TI reported revenue of $6.055B for the quarter, up 28% YoY and 11% sequentially, with Analog at $4.911B (+32%) and Embedded Processing at $858M (+21%).

### 5. Data Center and AI Infrastructure Demand (New in This Refresh)
Data center has become a material, named demand driver rather than a rounding error.
