Copper Retirement Legacy-Estate Assessment

AT&T must discontinue copper-based services in 50% of wire centers outside California by end 2027, substantially all by end 2029, tied to the $4B savings target. The first problem is analysis: which applications, data flows, and customer records still hang off each wire center. That entry is grounded in a real, priced Improving offering: Code Explorer, a 2-Week Discovery Sprint ($16K-$32K) or 4-Week Assessment ($32K-$64K) delivering a knowledge graph, ETL catalog, migration plan, and delivery-ready backlog. Offering-grounding gate run 10/23; the prior "cutover factory" execution claim matched no real offering and was removed.
“You've got a 2027 deadline on half your wire centers and a $4 billion number attached to it. Before anyone can run that cutover, someone has to know exactly which applications, data flows, and customer records still hang off each wire center. We have a fixed-price, two-week discovery sprint that maps one cluster's estate and hands you a migration-ready backlog. Would one wire-center cluster be a useful place to start?”
Why lead here: it is the only play whose entry shape matches a real, priced Improving offering end to end (Code Explorer, per the CCO delivery guide), against a dated, funded public commitment. It produces the delivered reference that makes the strategic plays pursuable.
