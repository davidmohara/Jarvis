## 1. Background / History

**Tim Brackney** is the CEO of Springline Advisory, a role he has held since early 2024 when Trinity Hunt Partners, a Dallas-based growth-oriented private equity firm, recruited him to lead the newly formed platform. He brought with him over 25 years in professional services, most of them at RGP (Resources Global Professionals), where he served in progressively senior roles over 22 years — ultimately as President and Chief Operating Officer. He resigned from RGP effective October 2023 and moved to Springline shortly after.

At RGP, Brackney served as EVP for Revenue, SVP for the North Pacific region, and later President North America before being elevated to President and COO. RGP is a NASDAQ-listed professional services firm built on an on-demand consulting model — so Brackney has deep experience building and operating distributed professional services businesses at scale, managing large practitioner networks, and navigating the tension between operational standardization and partner autonomy. That experience maps directly onto what Springline is trying to do.

Before RGP, he spent seven years at PwC, where he reached senior manager level. He holds an MBA from Stanford Graduate School of Business.

His public voice positions him as a people-first operator with a clear technology thesis. He has written and spoken explicitly about technology deficits in the financial services and accounting sectors, calling out why firms in this space lag in adoption — and framing PE-backed platforms like Springline as uniquely positioned to close that gap at speed. He has stated publicly: "We stay close to software leaders in our space, adopt new and even beta-stage tools early, and draw clear boundaries around what we adopt versus what we build." He has also said there is "such a broad intersection between the application of AI and hyper automation itself" — signaling he has already formed a view, not just curiosity. This is a buyer who understands the strategic stakes of AI and is not starting from scratch.

Brackney's leadership brand centers on culture as a competitive differentiator. He believes people-first culture is what makes professional services firms win the talent war. This is relevant context: David's role as Fractional AI Officer will involve working closely with a CEO who cares as much about how AI changes the employee experience as what it does to the P&L.

**Prior relationship with David / Improving:** This meeting was organized by David, with Devlin Liles as co-presenter and proposal author. The proposal has already been developed — this is not an early-stage intro call. Treat it as a proposal review and close conversation.

## 2. Company Overview

**Springline Advisory** is a PE-backed accounting and business advisory platform built through the acquisition and integration of regional CPA and advisory firms. Trinity Hunt Partners formed the entity in late 2023 with a seed firm — MarksNelson, an IPA 200-ranked accounting firm headquartered in Kansas City — and has since expanded aggressively through additional partnerships and acquisitions.

As of mid-2026, Springline reports approximately $165 million in revenue, 86 partners, and over 770 professionals across 14 offices. Accounting Today ranked it the 4th fastest-growing firm in 2025, with year-over-year revenue growth of 87.6%. Recent additions include SD Mayer (San Francisco, accounting/advisory/wealth), CG Advisory (New Jersey), EFPR Advisory (Rochester, NY), Christy Paddock Advisors, Fiske Advisory, and HM&M Advisory. The platform's stated run-rate context in the engagement proposal is $200–220M across twelve acquired firms, consistent with the pace of growth.

The firm operates through an alternative practice structure, meaning attest and non-attest services are separated across independently managed CPA entities under the Springline brand umbrella. This federated structure is central to the AI challenge: integrating AI across independently-governed firms, each with its own existing technology, workflows, and culture, is materially harder than deploying AI in a single unified organization.

**PE context:** Trinity Hunt Partners manages over $2 billion in AUM and focuses on growth-oriented middle-market business and consumer services companies. Springline is a roll-up thesis: acquire regional firms, consolidate under a shared brand and operating infrastructure, and create a platform valuable enough for a secondary transaction or exit. The AI decisions Springline makes in the next 18 months will directly shape the story told to the next capital partner — either as a capability differentiator or as a risk.

Critically: **Trinity Hunt Partners is also Improving's PE backer.** Springline is a sister portfolio company in the same fund. This is not an arm's-length sales call — it is a conversation between two companies that share the same capital partner. That shared context changes the tone, the trust level, and the commercial dynamics materially. Trinity Hunt has a direct interest in both companies succeeding, which means this engagement has implicit sponsor support that a standard prospect conversation would not have. David should be aware of this and comfortable referencing it if it surfaces.

**Technology posture:** Springline is consolidating twelve acquired firms onto a single Microsoft 365 tenancy and a Snowflake data foundation. This is not a greenfield build — it is a messy, real-world integration across entities that may have had minimal IT governance before joining the platform. Brackney has acknowledged that financial services firms carry significant tech debt and that platforms like Springline need to modernize fast. The Microsoft 365 / Snowflake foundation aligns directly with Improving's stack and with the proposed engagement architecture (Azure/Microsoft Foundry + Snowflake, model-agnostic orchestration).

**AI maturity:** No public AI initiatives have been announced. The engagement conversation describes Stage 2 to 3 to 4 boundary work — meaning Springline has begun some AI experimentation but has not yet institutionalized it. Given the federated firm structure, AI governance and sequencing will be Brackney's first real test: how do you roll out AI consistently across a dozen independently-minded partner groups?

The IOS framework — 8 stages, 3 waves, decision gates, fail-fast budgets, evidence-gated tranches — is the kind of structure he respects and can defend to his board and to Trinity Hunt. The explicit ROI hypothesis requirement on every execution bid removes the "AI theater" risk that PE-backed CFOs hate. The exit-ready snapshot (Realized / In-flight / Optioned) is language built for the next diligence conversation. Every element of the proposal architecture speaks directly to the reality of operating inside a PE-backed platform.

**David's specific value.** David is not positioned as an outside consultant who will hand over a strategy deck. The Fractional AI Officer structure means David is accountable to outcomes, sits in the weekly cadence, and owns the investor narrative alongside Brackney. That is a different relationship than advisory retainer work, and it is the right model for a CEO who has been the COO — he will know the difference immediately.

**Improving as delivery infrastructure.** Devlin's proposal positions Improving's purpose-built delivery pods as the execution arm behind David's advisory function. This removes the "who builds it" question from the conversation. David brings strategy, sequencing, and board-ready narrative; Improving's practitioners bring Azure, Snowflake, and agentic delivery capability. The Microsoft and Snowflake stack alignment is a genuine credential, not a positioning claim — Improving is a certified Snowflake partner and the Microsoft 365/Foundry architecture is native to Improving's AI delivery practice.

**The shared PE angle.** Trinity Hunt backs both Improving and Springline. That is not a footnote — it is the foundation of this conversation. It reframes the conversation from vendor pitch to sister company collaboration: "We're both in the Trinity Hunt portfolio. The way I see it, a win for Springline on AI is a win for the fund." **The PE narrative hook.** Trinity Hunt needs Springline to be a differentiated asset at exit. AI-enabled accounting and advisory platforms are commanding premium multiples in today's M&A market. The bi-modal ROI structure — efficiency portfolio for hard cost savings, innovation portfolio for proposal velocity and win rate — gives Brackney the language he needs to frame AI as both a margin story and a growth story to his PE sponsor. This is the angle that should resonate most with a CEO who is acutely aware that his company's next chapter depends on the story he can tell at the next capital event.

## 5. Talking Points

**Open by grounding in his reality, not the proposal.** Brackney is a builder running a fast-moving integration platform. Start by acknowledging what he is actually navigating: twelve firms, one tenancy goal, a PE sponsor watching the clock, and an AI conversation that is unavoidable. Something like: "We built this proposal around what we heard — a federated platform that needs AI decisions made with discipline, not speed-for-speed's-sake. Before we walk through it, what's changed or come into focus since you last looked at this?" That signals David is operating as a partner, not a vendor closing a deal.

**Lead with the governance problem, not the technology.** The hardest part of AI for a federated CPA platform is not finding tools — it is deciding which tools, in what sequence, with what governance, across partner groups who all think their way is the right way. David's role as Fractional AI Officer is explicitly to own that sequencing and governance problem. That is the value that justifies a retainer, not the strategy slides.

**The PE alignment angle.** If Brackney is the right kind of COO-turned-CEO, he is already thinking about what the next Trinity Hunt conversation looks like. Frame the exit-ready snapshot explicitly: "One of the deliverables in Q1 is a board and investor narrative draft. That is not a nice-to-have — it is the document that determines whether your AI investment is a line item or a valuation multiplier in your next diligence packet." That sentence lands differently with a PE-backed CEO than with an independent firm owner.

**On the $20K/quarter number.** This is not expensive for what it covers — quarterly strategy cadence, weekly working sessions, governance baseline, roadmap, and investor narrative. If price sensitivity surfaces, the right response is not to discount but to anchor on the cost of the alternative: $500K experiments with no governance and no narrative. The fail-fast budget structure means execution costs are capped and hypothesis-driven; the advisory retainer is what makes that structure work.

**Probe questions worth having ready:**
- "Where are you in the M365 consolidation — single tenancy live, or still migrating firms?"
- "Which of the twelve firms is furthest along on AI adoption? Is there a lead firm you'd want to use as the pilot platform?"
- "What does Trinity Hunt's stance on AI look like — are they pushing for it, or is this a Springline-level initiative?"
- "Who internally is the day-to-day AI owner, or is that a role that does not exist yet?"
- "What is the board or investor narrative around AI right now — is it a question you're already fielding, or one you're anticipating?"

**Devlin's role.** Devlin built the proposal and owns the execution architecture. Let him carry the delivery pod structure and the technical architecture discussion. David owns the advisory framing and the commercial conversation. Division of labor should be clear before the call starts — do not let it blur in real time.

## 6. Landmines / Watch-Outs

**The federated governance trap.** Twelve acquired firms with independent partners means twelve potential veto points. If Tim raises "how do we get partner buy-in across the firms," this is the right question and it deserves a direct answer. David's advisory role includes exactly this: sequencing adoption so that wins in one firm create pull from the others rather than centralized mandates creating resistance. Have a clear point of view ready on this, because it is the hardest operational reality Springline faces.

**Scope creep expectations.** The $20K/quarter retainer covers advisory — not embedded delivery, not tool procurement, not vendor management. If the conversation drifts toward "can you also manage the M365 rollout," that is a separate execution bid with its own ROI hypothesis. Hold the line on the retainer scope while making clear that execution capacity exists through the pod model.

**The "we have someone internally" response.** If Tim surfaces that he has hired or is hiring an internal Chief AI Officer or AI lead, the Fractional Officer model needs to reframe quickly. The value shifts from filling a vacant seat to augmenting and accelerating someone who is likely understaffed and over-tasked. David as external Fractional AI Officer alongside an internal owner is a perfectly viable model — do not treat the internal hire as a disqualifier.

**Don't over-leverage the Trinity Hunt relationship.** The shared PE backer is an opener and a trust accelerant — not a closing argument. Use it to establish context and warmth, then let the proposal stand on its own merits. Leaning too hard on "we're in the same fund" can read as pressure rather than partnership.

**RGP habits.** Brackney spent 22 years at a public company where everything was documented, measured, and reported quarterly. He will expect rigor, not just vision. The IOS framework and the evidence-gated tranche structure were built for exactly this kind of buyer — but make sure the proposal materials reflect that rigor on paper, not just in conversation.

**Timing pressure.** If Trinity Hunt is running any kind of timeline toward a capital event or secondary, AI strategy work that has not started in 2026 may not make it into the diligence narrative in time. Surface this gently if the conversation stalls on decision timing: "The Q1 deliverables exist specifically so you have a narrative ready before you need it, not after."

**Do not get lost in the technology stack.** Brackney is not an engineer. Azure/Microsoft Foundry and Snowflake are the right architectural choices and they align with his existing infrastructure direction — mention the alignment once and move on. The conversation that matters to him is governance, sequencing, ROI, and investor story. Keep it there.
