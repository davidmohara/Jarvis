## PriceSmart's Stated Strategic Priorities

Sourced from PriceSmart's FY2025 Form 10-K (filed October 30, 2025), the fiscal Q3 2026 Form 10-Q (filed July 8, 2026), the Q2 and Q3 FY2026 earnings releases (April 8, 2026 and July 8, 2026), the Q3 FY2026 Investor Presentation (July 2026), the September 1, 2026 press release, and PriceSmart's own investor relations materials. Five themes.

**PriceSmart states its own growth framework as three drivers**, and both the Q3 FY2026 10-Q and the Q3 FY2026 Investor Presentation use this exact framing: (1) invest in adding new PriceSmart locations, expanding into new markets, remodeling current clubs, and opening more distribution centers; (2) increase membership value; (3) drive incremental sales via PriceSmart.com and enhanced digital and technological capabilities. Themes 1 through 5 below map onto that framework. Note that **technology is not a standalone stated priority for PriceSmart**; it is presented as an enabler nested under the third driver alongside digital commerce. That is a positioning constraint worth respecting rather than arguing with.

### 3. Supply Chain and POS Technology Modernization (status revised 2026-10-23)
The FY2025 10-K describes substantial progress migrating to the **RELEX** supply chain platform, with full implementation targeted for FY2026, stated to "enhance employee productivity" and "improve inventory management, reduce spoilage and increase in-stock availability, driving both sales and efficiency." In parallel, PriceSmart is rolling out the **Elera** point-of-sale system across its English-speaking Caribbean markets (Q1 FY2026) and Spanish-speaking Central American markets (through FY2026), alongside new distribution-center capacity in China and multi-club markets.

**What changed and why this theme needs re-reading.** The FY2026 fiscal year ended **August 31, 2026**. The "full implementation targeted for FY2026" and "Elera rollout through FY2026" language therefore describes a window that has now closed, and **neither the Q3 FY2026 10-Q nor the Q3 FY2026 Investor Presentation repeats the RELEX or Elera implementation language**. The plan cannot continue to assert that PriceSmart is mid-rollout. What is confirmed:
- **RELEX is in production and has been expanded.** PriceSmart selected RELEX in **October 2023** for forecasting, replenishment and promotion planning across 52 clubs, then extended it in **January 2024** to pricing and promotion optimization, replacing a self-built pricing process. RELEX is therefore an installed, expanding platform, not an in-flight migration.
- **Both original RELEX sponsors have moved on.** David Price, who was quoted as Chief Transformation Officer on both RELEX announcements, is now CEO. Wayne Sadin, the CIO who fronted RELEX publicly, separated from PriceSmart on **July 1, 2026**. Shweta Bhatia inherited the platform.
- **Elera status is unverified as of this refresh.** No public confirmation of completion, expansion or cancellation was found. Flagged for verification against the FY2026 10-K rather than assumed either way.

## Entry Plays / Opportunity Scenarios

### Option A: Buildout and Rollout Delivery Capacity (revised 2026-10-23)
**This option has been re-scoped because its original premise expired.** In August it read "PriceSmart is mid-implementation on two major platforms (RELEX supply chain, Elera POS) with a full FY2026 completion target." FY2026 ended on August 31, 2026, RELEX is confirmed in production and already expanded into pricing and promotion planning, and neither the Q3 10-Q nor the Q3 investor presentation repeats the implementation language. Pitching a RELEX implementation would now be pitching something that is finished, which is the fastest way to lose credibility with a new CIO.

The live, dated, funded program is the buildout. **Five clubs and two distribution centers are in flight through spring 2027**, specifically Montego Bay, Jamaica (October 2026), South Camp Road, Kingston (winter 2026-27), a Jamaica in-country distribution center (fiscal 2026), Villa Nueva, Guatemala (winter 2027), Santo Tomas de Santo Domingo, Heredia (spring 2027), Comuna Las Condes, Santiago (spring 2027), and a Dominican Republic in-country distribution center (fiscal 2027), with a Florida Global Distribution Center consolidation and Cold DC relocation running in parallel. Each site needs POS, inventory, replenishment, member data and reporting stood up against an existing platform estate, on a fixed opening date. That is a delivery-capacity problem with a deadline on every unit.

**Grounding:** Data Enablement Engagement (Data Enablement category; Duration "4 - 8 weeks"; Price "$125,000 - $250,000"; source: `Sales Offerings/Specific Offerings/Data Enablement/Data Enablement Engagement.docx`). The gate run on 2026-10-23 reshaped this play: the catalog has no "rollout delivery capacity" offering, so the scoped, sellable entry is a 4-to-8-week Data Enablement Engagement anchored to one dated opening (Montego Bay is the nearest), delivering the member-data, replenishment and reporting datasets that site needs, with the multi-site program as the expansion path. The broader embedded-capacity relationship remains real and CRM-proven, but it is a delivery model, not a catalog offering, and is no longer presented as one.

**Entry pitch:** "You have five clubs and two distribution centers opening between now and spring 2027, each one needing the full systems stack stood up against a fixed opening date. We would start with one: a focused, four-to-eight-week data enablement engagement that stands up the member, replenishment and reporting data for a single opening, on a fixed price, and hands your team the pattern. Where is the capacity gap on that program right now, and which opening is the one keeping you up at night?"

### Option B: New-Market Systems Onboarding, Repeatable Playbook (Chile and beyond)
Each new-country club opening requires rapid systems onboarding (POS, inventory, member data, reporting) into existing platforms. The Chile entry (Mallplaza Los Dominicos, Comuna Las Condes, Santiago, spring 2027) remains the flagship case: PriceSmart's first new country in years and its first mall-format club. The refresh strengthens this option rather than weakening it, because Chile is no longer a single event. PriceSmart has disclosed executory agreements to acquire land for **two additional potential clubs**, so a market-entry systems playbook has repeat value, and the four clubs opening before Chile give Improving a chance to prove the pattern first.

**Grounding:** Platform Modernization Enablement (Platform Modernization category; Duration "4 - 12 weeks"; Price "$35,000 - $225,000"; source: `Sales Offerings/Specific Offerings/Platform Modernization/Platform Modernization Enablement.docx`). This is the closest real offering to a repeatable-onboarding play, and the match is specific rather than loose: its stated deliverables include "Replication Roadmap Development," defined in the source doc as "a clear and detailed roadmap for replicating these processes and tool integrations for additional applications," which is exactly the build-it-once-then-replicate shape this option sells. Caveat stated plainly: the offering is written for application/platform modernization, not country-entry logistics, so the club-opening framing is an adaptation of the real offering, and the full multi-market playbook ambition extends beyond its stated scope.

**Entry pitch:** "Chile is your first mall-format club and a brand-new market, and it is one of six openings you are running at once. If the systems-onboarding playbook you build for those six became the standard for every future market, what would need to be true about it?"

### Option C: Digital/Omnichannel Engineering Capacity
Digital sales are growing 20%+ YoY and the company is migrating to native iOS/Android mobile architectures. **Refreshed:** omni-channel penetration is 6.9% of net merchandise sales in Q3 FY2026 (6.6% for the nine months), the app runs alongside a live **UberEats** delivery channel rated 4.8 stars, **Click & Go** click-and-collect is a named core capability, and online membership onboarding and renewal is in scope. That is three integration surfaces plus an app rebuild sharing one member record.

**Grounding:** Application Modernization (Application Modernization category; Summary: "This engagement aims to assess the current state of the client's applications and provide a roadmap for modernization"; Duration/Price not stated in the source doc; source: `Sales Offerings/Specific Offerings/Application Modernization/Application Modernization - Internal Delivery Guide.docx`), which covers the native app rebuild at assessment-and-roadmap scope, paired with Data Enablement Engagement for the shared member record underneath the four channels. The prior version of this play leaned on a "Software/Product Engineering, Mobile Engineering" capability label that has no matching catalog offering; that label is removed.

**Entry pitch:** "Omnichannel just hit 6.9% of merchandise sales and you are rebuilding the app natively at the same time you are running UberEats, Click & Go, and online membership renewal off the same member record. Where is engineering capacity tightest against that roadmap?"
