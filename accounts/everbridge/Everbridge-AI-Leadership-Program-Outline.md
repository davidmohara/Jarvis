## The Problem We're Solving

Everbridge has executive mandate, tooling, and growing internal capability. What they don't have yet is 70 senior leaders who can build. The October Windsor offsite is a hard deadline with a specific ask: every leader shows up with a working AI solution they built and can explain.

That's not a training problem. It's a transformation problem with a deadline.

The risk isn't that leaders won't learn — it's that they'll learn plenty and still not build anything. The program has to be designed around the build, not the curriculum.

Cara's additional ask matters: this can't just be "here's how to use AI tools." Leaders need to leave knowing how to bring their teams along — how to reshape workflows, set new expectations, and lead adoption when they get home from Windsor.

---

## Design Principles for This Engagement

1. **The build is the curriculum.** Every session, every coaching touchpoint exists to advance a leader's specific AI solution. Not hypothetical use cases — real ones, owned from day one.
2. **Leaders, not developers.** No coding required. The tools are GPT builders, workflow automators, and agent platforms accessible without engineering. The skill is judgment, not syntax.
3. **Two outcomes, not one.** By Windsor, each leader has (1) a working AI solution and (2) a plan for how they lead their team through adoption of it. Both get showcased.
4. **Cohort accountability.** Peer pressure is the program's engine. Leaders share progress, blockers, and early builds with each other — not just with Improving coaches.

## Engagement Structure

### Pre-Program: Leader Intake + Cohort Assignment (Late June)
*Before Session 1*

Each participating leader completes a structured intake covering their domain, biggest workflow friction point, current AI comfort level, and an initial hypothesis for what they might build. Improving reviews intakes and assigns leaders to cohort groups of 12–15, deliberately mixed by function to encourage cross-pollination.

**Output:** Cohort assignments confirmed, cohort coaches assigned, leaders briefed on Session 1.

---

### Session 1: Shared Foundation (4 Hours, July)
*All ~70 leaders together — the one time the full group is in the same room (or virtual room)*

This is the program's anchor. Everyone goes through it together because the shared language built here is what makes the coaching sessions and Windsor showcase work. Leaders who use the same vocabulary for prompting, use cases, and AI limitations can give each other useful feedback. Leaders who don't, can't.

This is not an AI survey or executive overview. It's a working session. Leaders interact with AI tools throughout — building, not watching.

**Hour 1: How AI Actually Works (for Leaders)**
No engineering. The concepts leaders need to think clearly about what AI can and can't do: how models reason, where they fail, why hallucination happens, what "context" means in practice, and how the tools Everbridge already uses (OpenAI, Gemini, internal LLM assistant) fit together. The goal is a shared mental model, not technical depth.

**Hour 2: The Language of Building**
The practical vocabulary the rest of the program runs on — prompt engineering as a discipline, the difference between a GPT, a workflow automation, and an agent, how to think about inputs and outputs, and what "good enough" means at each stage of a build. Leaders practice live with their own problems, not canned examples.

**Hour 3: Finding Your Build Target**
Structured individual and small-group work. Each leader maps a real workflow friction point from their domain, stress-tests it as an AI use case (is this actually a good fit?), and begins narrowing toward a build hypothesis. Improving coaches facilitate. Peers react and challenge.

**Hour 4: Commitments and Cohort Launch**
Each leader states their initial build hypothesis to their assigned cohort group. Cohort coaches give first-pass feedback. Leaders leave with a build direction, a cohort, and a coach — not just a workbook.

**Output:** Every leader has a shared language for AI, a scoped build hypothesis, and an assigned cohort and coach. The program has a common foundation to build on.

---

### Office Hours: July through September
*A pre-set schedule of group sessions and optional 1:1s between Session 1 and Windsor*

After Session 1, the support structure shifts to Office Hours — a defined set of touchpoints leaders can join based on where they are and what they need. Every session is on the calendar from day one. Leaders choose which to attend; nothing is mandatory except the Windsor rehearsal. Improving coaches facilitate every session.

Office Hours come in three forms:

**Topical Sessions** *(60 min each — open to all ~70 leaders)*
Scheduled group sessions on a specific subject. Each one addresses a challenge that predictably surfaces at that stage of the build process. Leaders come with their work-in-progress and leave with a next step. Suggested topics across the three months:

- *Session OH-1 (Late July):* Use Case Stress Test — is what you're building actually the right problem? How to sharpen or pivot your hypothesis before investing too much time in the wrong direction.
- *Session OH-2 (Early August):* Prompting for Your Use Case — applied prompt engineering for the specific types of builds in the room (GPTs, automations, agents). Not a repeat of Session 1 foundations — this is about refining real work.
- *Session OH-3 (Late August):* Leading the Change — how to bring your team along when you land back from Windsor. Managing resistance, evolving workflows, setting new expectations. Each leader drafts a one-page team adoption plan.
- *Session OH-4 (Early September):* Build Clinic — open working time with coaches. Leaders share current state, surface blockers, and get direct input. No agenda other than unblocking.

**1:1 Sessions** *(30 min — bookable by any leader, any time July–September)*
Each Improving coach carries a standing block of availability for individual sessions. Leaders book when they hit a genuine blocker, need a design review, or want direct input on their build before a group session. Not a requirement — available to anyone who needs it.

**Windsor Rehearsals** *(30 min per leader — September)*
Every leader books a rehearsal slot in September. They run through their Windsor presentation — 5-minute build demo plus 2-minute team adoption plan — with a coach. Coach gives direct feedback on clarity, impact, and what to cut. This is the one non-optional touchpoint between Session 1 and Windsor. No one arrives unrehearsed.

**Output:** 70 leaders supported at their own pace, with structure for those who need it and flexibility for those who don't. Coaches have a read on every leader's build state before Windsor through rehearsal sessions.

### October: Windsor Showcase + Leadership Session (On-Site, UK)

#### Full Day Agenda — Suggested Framework

This is designed as a full or near-full day at the Windsor offsite, split into two acts: the showcase in the morning and the leadership session in the afternoon. Exact timing is a placeholder — Improving would work with Cara to fit within whatever the offsite schedule allows.

---

**ACT 1: THE SHOWCASE** *(Morning — approx. 3.5–4 hours)*

**8:30 AM — Opening: Why This Matters**
*(15 min — Cara Antonacci + David O'Hara)*
Brief framing of the journey: what the 70 leaders committed to in July, what Improving facilitated over 3 months, and what today represents. Sets the tone — this is a celebration of real work, not a conference session.

**8:45 AM — Showcase Round 1: Cohort A**
*(60–75 min)*
First cohort presents their builds in the main room. Format per leader:
- 5 minutes: live demo or walkthrough of their AI solution — what it does, who it helps, what problem it solves
- 2 minutes: team adoption plan — what they're doing with it when they land back home
- 1 minute: one thing they'd do differently if starting over

Improving facilitates, keeps time, and captures patterns on a visible wall/board.

**~10:00 AM — Break + Networking**
*(15 min)*
Leaders interact with the builds informally — "show me that again" conversations encouraged.

**10:15 AM — Showcase Round 2: Cohort B**
*(60–75 min)*
Second cohort presents in the same format.

**~11:30 AM — Cross-Cohort Patterns**
*(30 min — Improving-facilitated)*
Improving synthesizes what was just heard across both cohorts: common themes, unexpected applications, standout approaches. This is not a ranking — it's a sense-making exercise that surfaces what Everbridge leaders collectively discovered about AI.

Discussion questions:
- What use cases appeared across multiple functions that no one expected?
- Where did leaders struggle in common — and what does that tell us?
- What's the most replicable build in this room?

**12:00 PM — Recognition**
*(15 min)*
Cara (or the CEO if present) recognizes the cohort and individual standout builds. Not a competition — recognition for effort, creativity, and team impact.

**ACT 2: LEADING THE NEXT WAVE** *(Afternoon — approx. 2.5 hours)*

This is the live learning session Cara specifically asked for. It draws directly on what was built and shown in the morning — not a generic AI leadership talk.

**1:15 PM — From Builder to Leader: What Shifts Now**
*(30 min — David O'Hara + Improving facilitator)*
You've built something. Now the harder question: how do you lead your team to do the same? This session opens with a candid conversation about what the morning revealed — not just the builds, but the leadership moves leaders made (and didn't make) to get there. What held people back? What accelerated them?

**1:45 PM — Team Adoption in Practice**
*(45 min — workshop format, table groups)*
Leaders work in cross-functional table groups — mixed from different cohorts. Each table takes one real adoption challenge surfaced from the morning (Improving selects 3–4 genuine ones from the showcase debrief) and works through it using the change leadership framework from Session 4.

Tables present their recommendation to the room in 3 minutes. Brief discussion follows each.

**2:30 PM — Building an AI-Ready Team: The Leader's Playbook**
*(30 min — Improving-led)*
Concrete framework: what the best AI-forward leaders do differently. Covers team structure considerations, how to set new performance expectations, how to recognize and reward adoption, and how to handle the people who won't engage.

**3:00 PM — Commitments and Next Steps**
*(30 min — Cara-led, Improving supporting)*
Each leader states one public commitment: what they will do in their first 30 days back. Written, shared with the room, and optionally tracked by Cara's team.

Improving closes with a brief on what support looks like post-Windsor: sustaining the momentum, avoiding the "offsite high" that fades by week 3.

**3:30 PM — Close**

## Technology — Known, Assumed, and Open

### What We Know (from the call)

Everbridge has more tooling in place than most clients at this stage:

- **Enterprise OpenAI** — confirmed in use across the organization
- **Gemini and AWS** — active pilots, scope unclear
- **"Everbridge Profile"** — internal LLM interface launching June 2026; fed from their intranet; includes chat, automation, and agent features. This is their most relevant build platform and should be the primary tool for the program.
- **LinkedIn Learning** — AI fluency model already curating content by department and level

The internal LLM tool is significant. Leaders building on a platform the organization already owns, governs, and has data connected to is the right surface for this program — not personal ChatGPT accounts. David flagged this directly on the call: the builds need to be on sanctioned tooling so IT access, data governance, and long-term ownership are handled by the organization, not individuals.

### What We're Assuming (needs confirmation)

- Leaders have access to the Everbridge Profile by the time Session 1 runs in July
- Enterprise OpenAI licenses extend to all ~70 participating leaders (not just a subset)
- Everbridge's IT/security team has a process for approving new data connections in time for the build phase

### Open Questions — Technology Decisions Needed Before Proposal Finalizes

These need answers from Cara before the engagement can be fully scoped. They affect session design, build complexity, and what leaders can realistically accomplish in 3 months.

**1. Primary build platform: which tool are leaders using?**
The Everbridge Profile (internal LLM) is the strongest candidate — it has organizational data, IT sanction, and agent/automation features. But if it's not ready or accessible to all 70 leaders by July, Enterprise OpenAI (GPT Builder) is the fallback. This decision drives what leaders can build and how complex those builds can be. Needs a clear answer before Session 1 design is finalized.

**2. What data will leaders have access to?**
This is the question David flagged on the call as the one most likely to surface organizational impediments. The builds are only as good as the data behind them. Improving needs to know: Can leaders connect their builds to internal Everbridge data (CRM, operational systems, HR data, etc.)? Is there a governed process for requesting data access? Who approves it and how long does it take? The pre-program intake should include a data access question so blockers surface before Session 1, not during the build phase.

**3. Where do the builds live after Windsor?**
This is the long-term ownership question. Three options, each with different implications:

- *On the Everbridge Profile* — ideal. Builds stay inside the organization's governed environment, can be shared across teams, and the internal AI team can maintain them. Requires IT to provision a space for leader-built tools within the platform.
- *On Enterprise OpenAI (individual accounts)* — lower friction to start, but builds are siloed by leader and may not survive role changes or offboarding. Not a sustainable home.
- *On a team/department workspace* — depends on Everbridge's Microsoft 365 or other collaboration setup. Could work if IT defines a standard.

The answer matters for the Windsor showcase design too — if builds live on the Everbridge Profile, the showcase can include live demos from a single environment. If they're scattered across personal accounts, logistics get messier.

**4. What is the internal AI team's role?**
The AI team launching in June is a natural partner for this program — they understand the platform, governance, and data landscape better than anyone. Improving should understand whether they're positioned as a resource for leaders, as participants in the program, or as separate from it entirely. Involving them early avoids the scenario where leaders build things the AI team later has to undo.

## Open Questions — Engagement Design

1. **Cohort grouping:** By function, by level, or deliberately mixed? Mixed is recommended for the peer learning dynamic.
2. **Build constraints:** Are leaders free to pick any use case, or should builds tie to an Everbridge strategic priority?
3. **Windsor time allocation:** How much of the offsite day does Cara want to allocate to Improving? The Windsor agenda above assumes a full day.
4. **Budget range:** Determines the number of Office Hours topical sessions included, whether 1:1s are capped per leader, and how many Improving facilitators travel to Windsor.
