# Executive Assistant Coverage: The One-Page Case

**Recommendation:** Sustain Alice Mburu's Executive Assistant coverage. The data shows a full-time workload at a fraction of the cost of the alternatives.

---

## The quarter in numbers (Jul 5 – Oct 10, 2026, measured in Monday.com)

- **552 work items completed**, 82% of everything tracked on the executive work board
- **~1,500 messages** of daily operational traffic handled (≈25 per workday)
- **~65 daily briefings** delivered, every workday without exception
- **10+ travel itineraries, 6+ events, 12+ podcast guests, 81 published posts**
- **~450–600 hours** of work performed, i.e. **32–43 hours per week: a full-time role**

## What the work actually is

Not clerical support. Four jobs in one: an **operations manager** (travel with judgment calls, event execution, expense cycles, a $900 insurance dispute she drove to resolution), a **chief-of-staff layer** (inbox triage, calendar defense, intercepting issues before they reach the president), a **growth engine operator** (podcast guest pipeline, thought-leadership publishing at ~6 posts/week, CRM hygiene feeding pipeline visibility), and a **trustee of commitments** (YPO board compliance, relationship cadence, 16 dormant contacts re-engaged).

She also designed and runs the Monday.com tracking system this analysis is measured on.

## What it costs

**$3,350 per month ($40,200 annualized)** through Athena, a flat subscription for full-time, 1:1 executive assistant coverage: **~$19.40 per hour** for a workload measured at 32–43 hours per week.

## The alternatives

**Absorb it into the president's calendar:** 35+ hours per week of president-level time. At a conservative $200–300/hour, that is **$350k–500k annualized** against a $40k subscription: a **9–12x return** before counting a dollar of preserved revenue time. The math is not close.

**Redistribute across the team:** The work fragments across 4–6 people, none of whom hold the accumulated context: calendar memory, travel preferences, vendor relationships, the history of every chase. ~90 third parties already address Alice directly, keeping the president out of scheduling loops entirely. Removing her re-opens every one of those loops and adds 20–30% coordination overhead, plus months of ramp-up.

**Stop doing it:** Within a quarter the publishing cadence dies (81 posts of momentum decays), the podcast stalls, relationship and CRM cadence go quiet, YPO compliance lapses, and personal/medical logistics degrade first because they are the first things a busy executive drops.

## Bottom line

One role is currently absorbing the operational load of a president's office at EA cost, with zero dropped threads across 552 completed items and 14 weeks of daily execution. Replacing that with the alternatives costs more, delivers less, or loses the cadences entirely.
