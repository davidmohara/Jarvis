# The Case for Alice: ROI Analysis of Executive Assistant Coverage

**Period analyzed:** July 5 – October 10, 2026 (14 weeks, ~70 workdays)
**Baseline:** Monday.com workspace creation (July 5, 2026). Tracking and the message record begin the same week, so this is a clean measurement window.
**Prepared:** October 10, 2026

---

## Executive Summary

Since we started tracking work in Monday.com on July 5, 2026, Alice Mburu has completed **552 tracked work items** out of 583 assigned to her, while sustaining a daily operational rhythm with David of roughly **25 Teams messages per workday** (~1,500 total) plus ~180 emails where she serves as the operational entry point.

Combining tracked tasks with the untracked daily rhythm (inbox triage, daily recaps, vendor chasing), her workload models out at **31–42 hours per week: effectively a full-time role**, and the work is not clerical. It spans travel logistics with judgment calls, event execution, a podcast guest pipeline, a thought-leadership publishing cadence of ~6 posts per week, CRM hygiene, expense cycles, YPO board compliance, and personal/medical logistics that protect David's health schedule.

**The ROI argument in one line:** redistribute this work and it either consumes 35+ hours per week of president-level time (hundreds of thousands of dollars annualized, taken directly from client and pipeline time), fragments across 4–6 people who lack the context and add coordination overhead, or silently stops, killing cadences that took a quarter to build.

## Work Categories, Summarized

### 1. Daily Operations Rhythm (Inbox Triage + Daily Recap)
The backbone that never appears in task counts. Every workday Alice triages David's inbox and posts a Daily Overview & Recap: tomorrow's attire guide, travel confirmations, medical appointment status, and what needs his decision vs. what she already handled. She surfaces only what needs him, pre-summarized (UTB policy approvals, YPO waivers, EIP deadlines, LinkedIn messages including an escalated harassment stream). ~20% of message traffic.

**Evidence:** "Quick 3-item inbox check... a few I have already handled for you" (Oct 5); "Attire guide: Jacket, you have a podcast recording in the afternoon" (Sep 2).
**Est. effort: 90–120 hours (~6–9 hrs/wk).**

## The ROI Case vs. Alternatives

### Alternative A: David absorbs the work
35+ hours per week of president-level time. Even valued conservatively at $200–300/hour, that is $7,000–10,500 per week of displaced leadership time, **$350,000–500,000 annualized**, and the true cost is higher because those hours come out of client, pipeline, and content time, not out of slack (there is no slack). More practically: it does not fit. The work arrives in 25 messages a day plus a standing queue; his calendar is already full. The work would be dropped, not absorbed.

### Alternative B: Redistribute across the team
The work fragments across at least 4–6 people (scheduling, travel, events, CRM, expenses, publishing), none of whom hold the context Alice has accumulated in one quarter: calendar memory, travel preferences, vendor relationships (Texans org, ACG, Vistage, Four Seasons, Performance Medicine), and the medical-vendor chase history. Evidence that she is the single entry point: **~90 third parties now address Alice directly, keeping David out of scheduling loops entirely** (Diana Stevens, Bethany Hilton, and external partners all route through her). Removing her re-inserts David into every one of those loops, which is the exact problem the role exists to solve. Coordination overhead on fragmented admin work typically adds 20–30% on top of the same hours, and ramp-up to rebuild vendor relationships and preference knowledge takes months.

### Alternative C: Stop doing the work
What silently dies within a quarter:
- **81 published posts**: the thought-leadership cadence stops, and audience momentum built over 14 weeks decays
- **The podcast**: 12+ guest pipelines stall; the show's consistency is its growth engine
- **Relationship cadence**: 16 dormant contacts in re-engagement triage go quiet again
- **YPO board compliance**: RSVPs, surveys, chapter visits, speaker bureau application lapse, which has visibility consequences in the organization
- **CRM hygiene**: contact logging stops, degrading pipeline visibility for the whole sales motion
- **Event execution and close-outs**: ROI tracking on events ends
- **$900-class billing disputes**: money left on the table
- **Personal/medical logistics**: his health schedule degrades first because it is the lowest-priority work he personally would drop

### The multiplier effects that don't show in hour counts
1. **Judgment calls made on his behalf**: flight timing protecting social commitments, hotel cost vs. noise tradeoffs, expense categorization, which requests warrant his attention. These are decisions, not tasks.
2. **Drafting in his voice**: the Hylant dispute email, CC Breakfast follow-ups, introduction emails, all pre-drafted for approval rather than written from scratch.
3. **Interception**: items resolved before they ever reach David (declined meetings, vendor chases, routine replies).
4. **She built the measurement system**: the Monday structure that now gives leadership visibility into executive workload is her design.

## Method & Data Notes

- **Sources:** Monday.com (Work board items owned by Alice, 9 operational boards, update comments), Microsoft Teams (1:1 chat, Alice + Bethany group chat, Friday Week Wrap-Up meeting chat), Outlook email between David and Alice, July 5 – October 10, 2026.
- **Counts are exact where stated:** Work board counts come from a full filtered pull (583 items, 500 + 83 across two pages). Message counts come from paginated search to exhaustion, well under the search cap; monthly splits may be off by a handful. Estimates marked with ~.
- **Category assignment** is keyword-based with a manual review pass; category counts are approximate within ±10%.
- **July's 245 completions** include the initial seeding of the system (existing tasks migrated in the first week). August–September (161, 121) are the honest steady state.
- **Hours are modeled, not measured:** per-item minutes are stated as ranges and use the midpoint (e.g., 20 min per scheduling item, 2.5 hrs per trip-equivalent, 90 min per event). Ranges are carried through rather than false precision.
- **What this cannot capture:** quality, speed of response (same-day in most observed threads), and the trust dividend of 14 weeks without a dropped thread on tracked work.
