## Phase 2: Diagnosis, Crux, and the Bad-Strategy Scan

### Diagnosis

**The demand for AI work in Improving's client base is arriving faster than the organization's ability to sell and deliver it, and the existing machinery converts that demand into the wrong thing.**

Three mechanisms produce this:

1. **BD sells resumes, not outcomes.** The existing motion sells skilled people and delivery capacity to known buyers through known channels. AI demand comes from different buyers (business executives, not IT managers), in a different shape (an exploratory problem, not a scoped project), proven a different way (a working POC, not a case study). BD cannot scope it, cannot price it, and defaults to what it can do: staff augmentation at consulting rates with an AI label. Every deal that lands that way teaches the client that AI is a body count, and teaches the bench nothing.
2. **The bench is ahead of demand but behind delivery independence.** There is real talent, mostly still learning, with no defined path from "learning" to "leading an engagement," no reusable assets, and no protected time: utilization pressure will pull every one of them back onto billable staff-aug work the moment the region gets tight. Capability without an organizational container dissipates.
3. **The company structure is federal, and AI talent is scattered across the states.** Improving's offices run as enterprises with presidents who own their P&L and their clients. Any central AI practice that owns clients directly is at war with that structure on day one, and matrix-style ambiguity (the Nokia trap) kills new ventures inside federal companies: the CEO says "somebody build an AI practice" and nobody has the authority to actually do it. But leaving AI talent scattered per-office means no compounding: no shared offers, no reusable kits, no bench development, no way to staff a Dallas-sized engagement from Houston talent.

**Diagnosis in one sentence:** AI demand reaches Improving through buyers and proof-standards the existing sales motion cannot serve, meets a scattered, learning bench with no organizational container, and the natural result is mis-sold work, dissipated talent, and a missed window, unless the selling, the delivery capability, and the federation structure are built as one connected chain.

*Test of the diagnosis:* if right, we expect to see AI work arriving through hero-consultant channels (David's personal relationships and workshops) rather than BD pipeline; AI deals priced as staff augmentation; AI-adjacent interest in the enterprise that never converts to bookings; bench people torn between AI ambitions and billable assignments. The first two are observable fact (GEHC came through David, the City of Atlanta motion runs through Scott's relationships). The third and fourth are what David and the presidents report, which is why the measurement action below comes first.

### The Crux

**The crux is the first five AI engagements delivered end to end by the bench, without David (or a hero consultant) on the field, sold by the new pre-sales capability, each one leaving behind a reusable delivery kit.**

The crux test, applied against the alternatives:

- *Why not "the structure" (hub-and-spoke design) as the crux?* Because structure is a decision, not a capability. Curtis and the presidents can ratify a charter in one session. A structure with nothing inside it is an org chart with a logo. Solve structure and nothing else, and you have federated nothing.
- *Why not "pre-sales" as the crux?* Pre-sales is buildable in a quarter: hire or convert one strong person, give them the offers. But pre-sales with nothing to sell and nothing to point at is proposal theater. Also, pre-sales is only needed *because* delivery proof and packaged offers don't exist yet; it is downstream of the crux.
- *Why not "revenue target" as the crux?* That is the goal, not the hard part. Naming it changes nothing.
- *Why not "the bench" alone as the crux?* Bench development is the slowest constraint, and it is why the crux is phrased as five engagements rather than one: one engagement can still be carried by a hero. Five cannot. Five forces the bench through the full cycle (scope, deliver, demo, hand off, write it down) and produces the assets that make engagement six cheap.

**If we solve everything else, the charter, the pre-sales hire, the metrics, the marketing, and no bench-led engagement completes, the strategy fails:** 2027 ends with a titled practice, an overhead line, a few hero projects, and the "leading edge" position owned by whoever actually shipped. If we can't do the crux, we shouldn't pretend the climb is on.

### Bad-Strategy Scan of the Input Itself

Three of the six constraints in the original brief are goals or aspirations, not strategy, and they survive only after being converted:

- **"Market position as leading edge" is an aspiration, full stop.** Every consultancy in America claims it. It earns its place only as a *mechanism*: leading edge as a byproduct of shipping first and publishing the proof, riding the content engine that already exists. If it enters the charter as a claim, it is fluff.
- **"Revenue targets plus influenced-revenue metrics" is a target, not an approach.** Worse, "influenced revenue" is the single most gameable metric in consulting, and Improving's own 2026 record proves the failure mode: the $15M co-sell target sat past deadline and undeclared dead for months, and two fabricated "CLOSED" co-sell figures were caught on Aug 31. The metric design must carry its own attribution rules and falsification tests or it becomes the next self-congratulation engine.
- **"Delivery-led + enablement engine" is a real strategic choice** (what we sell is proof and capability transfer, not headcount), and it is the strongest part of the brief. Keep it. It implies pricing and delivery economics the existing BD genuinely cannot handle, which justifies the pre-sales investment.

## Phase 3: The Kernel

### Guiding Policy

**Proof before promotion. The hub builds the engine; the spokes own the clients. Nothing is sold the bench cannot deliver, and nothing is claimed in the market that a named engagement cannot prove.**

In plain terms: a small central hub (David + pre-sales + a delivery architect + an enablement lead) owns the offer portfolio, the delivery kits, the pre-sales motion, bench development, and the revenue scoreboard. Each enterprise (the spokes, the presidents) owns client relationships, pursuit priority, and delivery staffing, and every AI engagement is delivered by spoke-based bench with a hub architect attached. The market position rides delivery proofs and David's existing thought-leadership engine, never marketing claims.

**What this policy rules out (a policy that eliminates nothing is a slogan):**

1. **No central client ownership.** The hub does not own accounts, does not hold its own client P&L, and does not compete with presidents for pursuits. Spokes own clients. The moment the hub starts chasing its own logo list, the federation is dead and the presidents will treat the practice as an adversary.
2. **Sell ahead of the bench, never ahead of a named staffed team — and every early engagement carries a bench shadow.** Pre-sales can sell capabilities that haven't been delivered yet; David is explicitly comfortable selling on hypotheticals and stretching. The two hard constraints that keep that safe: (a) nothing is signed without named staff attached — a hub architect or anchor hire who can actually deliver it, and (b) every early engagement, however staffed, has at least one cohort-1 bench consultant shadowing or pairing on it. The first delivery of any new offer is a lighthouse that produces the delivery kit; the second hardens it; the third enters the catalog. This keeps the speed David wants without the trap it creates: early revenue staffed entirely by heroes, which books 2027 bookings and leaves 2028 with nobody who can deliver but David.
3. **No staff-augmentation pricing for AI work.** AI engagements price as fixed-scope outcome/POC phases with defined deliverables. Anything that must be priced as bodies routes through the normal delivery business, not the practice.
4. **No mass external hiring in year 1 — but the anchor hires move to the front.** The bench is built by converting internal consultants (the Ready Room pipeline) plus two senior anchor hires. Because selling now runs ahead of the bench (exclusion 2), those anchors are no longer a nice-to-have for kits and mentorship: they are the staffing bridge for every early engagement, hired by Q1, with an explicit job description: deliver the stretch engagements, build the kit while doing it, and bring the paired bench consultant up behind them. Buying a whole bench teaches nothing and creates a rented capability the org never absorbs; two anchors bought early are the price of selling ahead of proof.
5. **No product or IP bets in year 1.** No building software to resell, no tooling startups inside the practice. Year 1 compounds delivery assets and reference proofs. Productization is a 2028 decision made on 2027 evidence.
6. **No blended revenue reporting.** Every direct and influenced-revenue figure is reported per enterprise, with written attribution rules, audited monthly. The One Texas lesson applies from day one: a metric that only looks good when aggregated is not good.
7. **No undefined calendar expansion for David.** The practice grows when engagements complete without David on the field. David's role is the charter, the pre-sales spine, the anchor relationships, and the market voice through the existing platform. If the 12-month plan requires David delivering, the plan has failed its own design.

**Why this policy has power:** it aims Improving's actual asymmetries at an affectable target. The asymmetries: (1) a working executive content platform that reaches buyers before competitors' sales motions do, (2) deep multi-year client trust (the thing that makes enterprises let you near their data and systems), (3) a federal structure that, properly chartered, turns every office into a distribution channel instead of a competitor. The target: regional enterprise clients (the existing account base) who want AI outcomes but cannot buy them from hyperscaler partners or Big-4 rates. A competitor can copy "AI practice." It cannot copy David's platform plus the incumbent client trust plus a federation that's actually ratified.

### Coherent Actions: The 12-Month Build Plan

Six actions, one spine, phased by quarter. Remove any one and the others weaken; the arrows are drawn at the end.

**A1. Charter and decision rights, signed before anything else.** *(Owner: David with Curtis, ratified with the president group. Target: Dec 1-2 session, signed by Dec 15, the Rock 5 date.)*
One page per element: hub/spoke ownership split (hub owns offers, kits, pre-sales, bench development, scoreboard; spokes own clients, pursuits, staffing), practice-level revenue definitions, the exclusions above, and the decider rules. The Nokia trap is the failure mode: this document must say who decides what when a president wants an AI proposal the bench can't staff (answer: pre-sales can propose only the catalog; new capabilities route to David for a lighthouse ruling) and when the hub wants a client intro the president won't give (answer: it goes on the president-group agenda, not around them). The Dec 1-2 session already has "2027 strategies" as its theme and Scott asked for agenda topics seven weeks ago: this is the topic. The charter enters that session.

**A2. Define the offer portfolio: three named offers, each with a delivery kit.** *(Owner: David with the practice delivery architect. Draft by Jan 15, hardened by Mar 31.)*
Not "AI services." Three concrete, sellable, phased offers, each one page: buyer, problem, what phase 1 delivers in how many weeks at what price shape, what "done" means, and the named reference (a reference can be a precedent engagement, not necessarily a completed one — selling ahead of delivery is sanctioned, so offers launch when the staffing plan exists, not when the proof does). Drawing on the real precedents: an Executive AI Enablement offer (Everbridge outline, DLP bootcamps, David's four delivered workshops; this one is already de-risked), an AI Readiness/Opportunity Assessment offer (the front door for enterprises; Amber's AI Policy work feeds the governance module), and a POC-to-Production offer (the GEHC AI Router pattern: 12-week paid proof, then a production phase). The GEHC outcomes being uncaptured is the first repair job: capture it, because it is currently the practice's only delivery proof and it is sitting unharvested. Offers go into the catalog on a staffing plan plus a delivery path, not on completion — and every catalog offer ships with a bench-shadow slot defined.

**A3. Stand up the pre-sales capability.** *(Owner: David. Named person in seat by Feb 1, productive by Mar 31.)*
One senior person, internal conversion preferred (someone who knows Improving's delivery model and can win a technical room), with three jobs: qualify AI demand across the enterprises, build and run the pursuit motion (executive framing, POC proposals, pricing), and feed every loss back into the offer portfolio. The mandate is wide on the front end and narrow on the back end: pre-sales can propose anything, including capabilities not yet delivered — that is the point of the stretch — but nothing converts to a signed engagement without David's lighthouse ruling (which confirms the named staffing plan and the bench-shadow slot). Measure this role on proposals sent and POCs closed, not on meetings held. This is the action that directly answers the "BD cannot sell it" constraint, and it must not become a second BD org: one person, then two, growing as the pipeline grows.

**A4. Build the bench through the conversion pipeline.** *(Owner: David with the enablement lead, coordinated with the Ready Room, per enterprise. Cohort 1 selected by Feb 1, five consultants.)*
Named internal consultants, one or two per enterprise, selected with their president's sign-off (spoke consent is what makes federation real). Each gets: a written development path (assessment, pairing on a lighthouse, then leading with a hub architect attached), protected allocation for year 1 (the president commits a percentage of their time that utilization pressure cannot claw back; without this, A4 dies silently in March), and a certification bar: "leads an engagement" is a demonstrated event, not tenure. Cohort 2 (five more) starts Q3. By year end: ten through the pipeline, at least five leading independently. Anchor hires (at most two) arrive to mentor and build kits, not to bill.

**A5. Run the enablement engine as a product, per enterprise.** *(Owner: enablement lead, David's voice for the flagship events. Full cadence live by Q2.)*
The engine that already exists becomes a scheduled product: a standing executive AI enablement series per enterprise (the DRC/Convergence/SMU pattern), a client-facing workshop offer that pre-sales can actually sell (the City of Atlanta Executive Claude Workshop is the template), and the internal bench curriculum. This is the second asymmetry in motion: the market position is built here, in front of buyers, not in a marketing campaign. David's speaking calendar and Alice's publishing engine feed it; the podcast becomes the practice's national voice. "Leading edge" is earned when a client hears the talk and then sees the working POC from the same firm.

**A6. Revenue scoreboard with attribution rules, live before the first dollar is booked.** *(Owner: David with the practice ops owner and finance. Definitions in the charter (A1), fields live in the scorecard by Feb 15.)*
Two tiers, reported per enterprise, never blended:
- **Direct revenue:** engagements sold by the pre-sales motion against the offer catalog, priced as outcomes/phases. Year 1 target set in the Curtis negotiation; the defensible shape is a first-year direct number that the named early plays could plausibly reach (the GEHC-pattern POC at its scale times the first five engagements, compounding into production phases). The falsification rule comes from the 2026 lesson: at the Q2 review, if direct pipeline coverage is under 2x the remaining target, the target is re-baselined in writing with a mechanism attached, not left to die quietly on the books.
- **Influenced revenue:** deals in any practice where the AI motion was the entry point or a scored component, credited only under written rules: named in the deal record by the selling president, AI activity documented pre-close, capped contribution percentages by role type (entry point vs component vs adjacent). Unattributed "AI-adjacent" revenue counts zero. The influenced tier exists because the strategy's honest promise is that AI opens doors for the whole firm; it must not become a vanity number, so it carries the strictest attribution in the company.

**The arrows:** A1 ratifies A6's definitions and A4's protected allocation; A2 gives A3 something to sell; A3 generates the engagements that exercise A4; A4's completions harden A2's kits; A5 feeds A3's pipeline and builds the market position that A2's pricing defends; A6 tells you by April whether any of it is working. It is one chain: charter → offers → sell → deliver → prove → resell.

- **Q1 2027:** A2 catalogs hardened; A3 person in seat; A4 cohort 1 selected and paired; A6 scoreboard live. Two lighthouse engagements in flight.

## Phase 4: Create-Destroy

Five kills, each with the response built in:

1. **The window closes.** By late 2027, "AI capabilities" is table stakes everywhere and every consultancy claims delivery. *Destroyed if:* the position rests on claims. *Survives because:* the position rides assets competitors can't copy quickly: David's platform, incumbent client trust, and referenceable bench-led proofs. The forcing function: the first reference-able client story ships by end of Q2 or the market-position objective is formally downgraded in the Q2 review. Leading edge is earned by shipping, and the falsification date is on the calendar.
2. **The federation collapses into the matrix trap.** Presidents starve the bench (utilization pressure, every region down MTD), the hub gets frozen out of pursuits, and the practice becomes a title. This is the most likely 12-month failure. *Response:* the protected-allocation commitment is in the charter itself (A1) with the president's signature, and the Q2 review grades each enterprise on bench time actually protected, not promised. If two quarters pass with allocation clawed back, the honest move is declaring the federation failed and proposing a concentrated structure to Curtis with evidence, not pretending the federation is alive.
3. **The bench plateaus, or the stretch succeeds without it.** Selling ahead of the bench works commercially — and creates the risk that every engagement gets staffed with anchors and David because that's faster than pairing a learning consultant. Twelve months of that books great revenue and produces a practice that cannot deliver without its founder. This is now the most seductive failure mode because it looks like success. *Response:* the bench-shadow rule is contractual: no signed engagement without a named cohort consultant on it, and "leading an engagement" is a dated, demonstrated certification event, cohort-checked quarterly. David's own calendar is the governor (exclusion 7): if David's delivery hours grow in 2027, the strategy is failing and it shows up in the review.
4. **Pre-sales becomes overhead theater.** One hire produces decks, meetings, and zero POCs; the "BD cannot sell it" constraint is restated rather than solved. *Response:* the role is graded on proposals sent and POCs closed per quarter, with a named kill-switch (two consecutive quarters under threshold = role redesigned).
5. **Influenced revenue becomes the next blended-reporting scandal.** Every practice starts claiming AI influence; the metric inflates; the scorecard lies; the Q3 2026 pattern repeats at practice scale. *Response:* attribution rules written in the charter, first audit in Q3, credited only when a president puts their name on the claim in the deal record. The Aug 31 fabrication incident is the standing reason this metric gets an auditor, not just a definition.

**The one-question test:** in 12 months, two causes of death now share the lead. #2, the federation, because it depends on people outside the practice choosing to make it work. And #3 in its new form — commercial success that quietly skips the bench — because it wears the costume of winning and won't be caught by revenue reviews. The charter, protected allocation, and president ratification address #2; the bench-shadow rule and David's calendar as governor address #3. Everything else is sequenced behind them.

## Phase 5: The Two-Minute Action Agenda

**Diagnosis:** AI demand reaches Improving through buyers and proof standards the existing sales motion cannot serve, meets a scattered learning bench with no organizational container, and will convert into mis-sold work and dissipated talent unless selling, delivery, and federation are built as one chain.

**Guiding policy:** Proof before promotion. The hub builds the engine (offers, kits, pre-sales, bench development, scoreboard); the spokes own the clients. Nothing sold the bench cannot deliver. Nothing claimed a named engagement cannot prove.

**Coherent actions:** (1) Charter with decision rights signed by Dec 15 via the Dec 1-2 president session; (2) three named offers with delivery kits by Mar 31; (3) pre-sales in seat by Feb 1, graded on POCs closed; (4) cohort-based bench conversion, protected allocation, five leading independently by year end; (5) enablement engine run as a product per enterprise; (6) two-tier revenue scoreboard with hard attribution rules live by Feb 15.

**The crux:** five bench-led engagements delivered end to end without David, sold by pre-sales, each leaving a reusable kit.

**Exclusions:** no central client ownership, no sales ahead of proof, no staff-aug pricing, no mass hiring, no product bets, no blended reporting, no David-as-deliverer.

**90-day success indicators (measured ~end of Q1 2027):**
1. Charter signed by Curtis and every president: structure, decision rights, allocation percentages, revenue definitions (due Dec 15; the Dec 1-2 session is the ratification moment).
2. Three offers cataloged with kits; GEHC outcomes captured as the first named reference.
3. Pre-sales lead in seat, first proposals out, first POC closed or in signature.
4. Cohort 1 (five named consultants) paired on live engagements, allocation protected in practice, not just promised.
5. Scoreboard live: direct and influenced revenue fields per enterprise, zero unattributed claims.
6. Two lighthouse engagements in flight, each with a dated path to "led by a cohort-1 consultant, not David."

**The standing falsification rule:** Q2 review, direct pipeline under 2x remaining target = honest re-baseline in writing. No dead targets, ever, per the 2026 lesson.
