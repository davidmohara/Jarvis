# Agents Don't Need Seats. But They Still Cost Money.

## Hook
OpenAI just moved workspace agents to token-based credit pricing. Cost now scales with what the
agent actually does, not whether it was invoked. That sounds like progress. It's also a new kind
of risk that most enterprise procurement teams haven't priced in.

## Story Angle
The Gartner $234B SaaS-at-risk number gets quoted a lot. What doesn't get quoted: the other side
of that equation. If your agents replace seat-licensed users, the seat cost goes away. But the
agent's token spend is now variable, usage-driven, and almost completely invisible to the budget
owners who approved the AI initiative. I've talked to finance leaders who have no idea what their
AI agents are spending. That gap is going to be a problem.

## Core Insight
The threat to SaaS vendors is real. But the threat to enterprise AI buyers is different: you've
traded a predictable cost (seats) for an unpredictable one (token consumption at scale).
Governance needs to cover the spend model, not just the behavior.

## Challenge / CTA
Has your organization built a financial governance model for agent token spend, or is it still
coming out of the "AI experimentation" budget line?
