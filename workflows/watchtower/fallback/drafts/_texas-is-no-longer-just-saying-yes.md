# Texas Is No Longer Just Saying Yes

## Hook
Abbott's July 17 deadline passed. The PUC and ERCOT delivered the joint memo. Data centers now
pay their own grid infrastructure costs. No more socializing to residential ratepayers. Meanwhile,
in the same week: Meta opened $1.2B in Temple, Nvidia/Wistron opened in Fort Worth, LITEON
announced $919M in McKinney. Texas is attracting the capital and writing the terms of the
relationship at the same time.

## Story Angle
For the last two years, every conversation I've had about Texas AI infrastructure ended with
"Texas says yes to everything." That frame is gone. The state is operating with a specific
posture now: private capital builds it, private capital pays for the grid to support it, and
Texas captures the jobs and tax base. That's a more sophisticated position than most people
outside Texas realize.

## Core Insight
For clients making long-term AI infrastructure bets in Texas, the policy clarity is actually good
news. Uncertainty about cost socialization was the bigger risk. Now you know the terms. The 450
GW of requested capacity against 85 GW of peak demand tells you there will be winners and losers
in the queue. Understanding those terms is table stakes for any regional client conversation.

## Challenge / CTA
If you're advising a client on Texas AI infrastructure investment in the next 18 months, what's
your read on the ERCOT queue priority rules and how they affect timing?
