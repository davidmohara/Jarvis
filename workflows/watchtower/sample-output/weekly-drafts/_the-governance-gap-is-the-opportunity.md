# The Governance Gap Is the Opportunity

## Hook
Gartner says 40% of enterprise applications will have task-specific agents by end of 2026. They also say more than 40% of agentic projects will be canceled by 2027. That's not a contradiction. That's a business.

## Story Angle
The failure mode Gartner is predicting isn't technical. It's governance. Agents running without audit trails, without least-privilege access, without anyone accountable when something goes sideways. I've watched this pattern before. It's the same reason ERP projects failed in the 90s and the same reason cloud migrations stalled in the 2010s. The technology works. Organizations don't yet know how to govern it. And when the first major agent failure hits a regulated industry and makes the news, the remediation engagements are going to go to whoever already has a playbook. That's the race I want to be in.

## Core Insight
The 40% cancellation rate Gartner is predicting is not a warning to avoid agentic AI. It's a market signal that governance-first deployment is an underserved offering. The firms that build that playbook now, before the failure headlines, are the ones clients will call when things go wrong. That's a better competitive position than racing to deploy the most agents the fastest.

## Challenge / CTA
Does your practice have a governance playbook for agentic AI? Not a capabilities deck. An actual framework clients can use before they're in trouble.
