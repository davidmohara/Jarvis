# Executive Presence Development Program (Working Title: "Boardroom Ready")

**Owner**: David O'Hara + Devlin Liles
**Status**: Design draft v2, October 2026

## Design Constraints

- Independent program: not tied to EDP, Executive Sales rollout, or the bootcamps.
- OJT / live-fire primary: real client meetings with real stakes are the core mechanism. Drills support live exposure; they never substitute for it.
- Self-sustaining by design: the machinery (assignment, capture, review) does the remembering, not the owners' calendars.

## Goal

Grow younger consultants into leaders who show up well in the C-suite, via an assignment structure modeled on MBB apprenticeship mechanics.

---

## 1. Core Concepts

### Answer-First / Pyramid Principle (Minto)

- Answer first: every communication opens with the governing claim.
- Three supports, MECE, each a claim (not a topic), never a chronology.
- Recursive: every slide, section, and verbal update has its own mini-pyramid.
- Slide titles are claims, not topics.
- Coaching heuristic: the 30-second test. Can the presenter stop at any point and deliver the whole story in 30 seconds?

### Executive Presence Components (each rated separately)

1. **Structure**: pyramid discipline.
2. **Concision**: time-boxed answers; land the point, stop talking.
3. **Composure under challenge**: hostile Q&A; disagree without flinching; "I don't know, I'll find out" delivered with confidence.
4. **Reading the room**: noticing reactions; adjusting altitude (exec vs. detail) mid-stream.
5. **Ownership**: "my analysis says X and I recommend Y," not hedged attribution to the team.

## 2. Candidate Criteria (selection gate)

| Criterion | What to look for | How to assess |
|---|---|---|
| Communication aptitude | Structures verbal answers reasonably; writes in claims, not just facts | Sample a written deliverable + one observed interaction |
| Drive for the room | Wants client-facing altitude; seeks feedback | Manager interview; prior feedback history |
| Composure baseline | Handles being wrong or challenged without deflating | Challenge them hard in the assessment conversation |
| Reliability under pressure | Delivers when the stakes are real | Track record on engagements |
| Coachability | Applies feedback within days, visibly | Reference from current engagement lead |

**Exclusion signals**: over-preparation as avoidance (hides behind slides), defensiveness under challenge, one-way communication (can't read the room), "I'm technical, presence isn't my job" framing.

**Selection mechanics**: nominated by engagement lead, interviewed by David or Devlin. Cohort size 4-6 to keep airtime real. Candidates not selected receive a development note on what to close.

## 3. The Airtime Ladder (the assignment structure)

Room time is assigned, never left to chance. Each rung is a gate, not a time period; advance when the rubric says so, roughly one rung per quarter.

| Rung | Assignment | Requirements | Gate to advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Observer** | Sits in C-suite/QBR meetings with a senior; owns the observation debrief | Post-meeting: answers "what did you see the exec react to, and why?" (oral, 5 min, to the senior) | 3 debriefs rated "sees the room" by the senior |
| **2. Section Owner** | Presents one section (5-10 min) of a client meeting; senior beside them | Preps pyramid-style; passes 30-second test in rehearsal; handles Q&A on own section | 2 sections rated 4+/5 on structure + composure; written presence note after each |
| **3. Module Owner** | Owns a full module or workstream conversation (20-30 min) | Writes the module's governing message; handles challenge without the senior rescuing; adjusts altitude on the fly | 1 module where the senior stayed silent through Q&A |
| **4. Room Owner** | Runs the meeting; owns exec relationship for a workstream | Opens with the answer; manages the clock and the politics; closes with decisions captured | 2 meetings run solo with senior debrief only after |

**Per-rung requirement**: the 30-second test must pass in rehearsal before any rung 2+ exposure.

### Rubric (5-point, used at every rung, scored by the senior in the room)

Each component rated 1-5 with one written observation as evidence:

| Component | 1 (developing) | 3 (competent) | 5 (boardroom ready) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | Topic-ordered narrative; answer buried | Answer up front, loosely supported | Tight pyramid; every layer is a claim |
| Concision | Over-explains, backfills | Lands the point, some ramble | 20-second answers; silence used well |
| Composure | Deflects or deflates under challenge | Holds, but visible strain | Welcomes challenge; "let me find out" with confidence |
| Room reading | Presents past objections | Notices, adjusts slowly | Adjusts altitude mid-sentence; names the unspoken question |
| Ownership | "We found..." hedging | "The analysis says..." | "I recommend X, here's why, and I'll own the outcome" |

Ratings must be anchored to a quoted moment ("when the CFO cut in at minute 12, you..."). Unanchored ratings do not count.

## 4. Drills (two modes)

The live meeting is the curriculum. Drills run in two modes:

**Mode A — Pre-meeting warm-ups** (15 min, before scheduled real exposure). Run against that meeting's actual content: 30-second test on the participant's section, one answer-first pass, one anticipated hostile question.

**Mode B — Situational drills** (ad-hoc, 10-30 min, any time). Run whenever a senior has a participant available: end of a work session, office time, cohort get-togethers. Also the assessment vehicle when a rung-advancement decision needs evidence beyond live meetings.

Drill list (all work in either mode):

1. **30-second test**: stop the presenter at a random point; deliver the whole story in 30 seconds.
2. **Answer-first**: one page of messy engagement facts; 5 minutes prep; 60-second "what would you tell the CFO."
3. **Slide title rewrite**: rewrite every title of a real past deck from topic to claim.
4. **Hot seat Q&A**: 3 minutes presenting, then 5 minutes of hostile challenge (a senior playing skeptical CFO). No hedging; "I don't know, I'll find out" is allowed and coached.
5. **Video review**: record a hot seat; participant watches own tape, silent critique first, then senior commentary.
6. **Altitude switch**: explain a technical concept at three altitudes, board / VP / practitioner, 60 seconds each.
7. **Unspoken question**: after a mock update, the presenter names what the exec is worried about but didn't ask, then answers it.

## 5. Feedback Capture System

Three artifacts:

**A. Presence Note** (after every rung 2+ exposure). Written by the senior within 24h, to the participant, cc'd to the cohort tracker. Two notes exactly: one content, one presence. Each anchored to a quoted moment.

**B. Ladder Card** (per participant, in the program tracker). One row per exposure: date, meeting, rung, rubric scores (5 components), gate progress. Single source of truth for advancement. Visible to the participant.

**C. Quarterly Review** (David + Devlin, per cohort, 30 min). Output: next quarter's airtime assignments by name and by meeting. A quarter ending with a participant at zero assigned exposures is a program failure logged against the owners, not the participant.

**Presence Note format** (fixed template; a note that doesn't fit the template doesn't count):

```
Presence Note — [participant], [date], [meeting], [rung]

CONTENT (one note):
[The single most important content observation, anchored to a quoted moment:
"when X said Y, your response Z..." — what to repeat, what to change]

PRESENCE (one note):
[The single most important presence observation, anchored to a quoted moment,
rated against the rubric component it maps to:
"when the CFO cut in at minute 12, you..." — component: composure (4/5)]
```

Rules for writers: two notes exactly, one content and one presence; each anchored to a specific moment, never a general impression; end with the next rung's single focus ("next time: pause before answering"). Under 150 words total. Written within 24h of meeting close.

**Storage**: ladder cards and presence notes in a shared SharePoint folder alongside Sales Offerings; quarterly review outputs appended to this project file. Use infrastructure consultants already open daily; no dedicated tooling at launch.

## 7. Incentive Wiring

Program scorecard per quarter: exposures delivered vs. assigned, rung advancements, participant retention in client-facing roles at 12 months. The quarterly review output is the owners' evidence; no separate reporting.

Airtime assignment failure counts against the program, not the participant. The participant's job is to show up prepared; the seniors' job is to make the room exist.

---

## 8. Self-Sustaining Mechanics

1. **Airtime is scheduled with the meeting, not before it.** When a senior books a client meeting with a cohort participant attending, the invite includes the assignment ("Section owner: intro + findings, 8 min"). The assignment travels with the calendar; it can only be consciously removed, not forgotten.
2. **Presence notes are triggered by meeting end.** The senior's note obligation starts at meeting close. Notes more than 48h overdue escalate to the quarterly tracker as a program-miss, visible to the owners.
3. **Ladder cards are the program's memory.** A participant with 30 days of no scheduled exposure is visible on the card; this is the early-warning signal.
4. **The quarterly review outputs the next quarter's assignment sheet.** If the review happens, the next quarter runs until the following review. One meeting per quarter is the only human-push requirement.
5. **Two owners, alternating chair.** David chairs one quarterly review, Devlin the next.
6. **Optional monthly check** (not required for launch): 15-minute ladder-card scan between quarterlies for stalled participants; can be run by Alice as a standing task.
