## CLIP 2 of 5: A3: "The Billable Hour Just Met Its Match"

**Length: 90s (~230 words) · Timely: ties to Episode 17 release week.**

**HOOK:**
"Work that used to bill ten hours now takes one. Somebody's invoice is about to get very uncomfortable."

**BODY:**
"I'm not guessing at this. We do this work. Contract review that took a team of associates a week comes back before lunch. Research memos, discovery summaries, first drafts: the hours are collapsing everywhere we look.

Here's what didn't collapse: the judgment. Knowing which clause actually matters, what the other side is really asking for, when to pick up the phone. That was always the product. The hours were just how we packaged it.

So if your business model bills for time, AI isn't coming for your people. It's coming for your pricing. Your best people become more valuable and your invoice becomes harder to defend, at the same time.

The smart firms are repricing themselves before their clients do it for them. New models, flat fees, value pricing, whatever fits. The firms that wait will have the conversation anyway. It'll just be their client's procurement team leading it.

If your outside counsel hasn't brought this up yet, maybe you should. Their reaction will tell you a lot."

## CLIP 3 of 5: C4: "Three Questions I Ask Every Executive"

**Length: 90s (~230 words) · Evergreen.**

**HOOK:**
"Before I let a leadership team spend a dollar on AI, I ask them three questions."

**BODY:**
"Question one: what decision does this make faster or better? Not what tool do you want to try. What decision. If the answer starts with a product name, we start over.

Question two: where does your data actually live, and would you bet a real decision on it? Everybody says yes to that one. Then we go look. It's in fourteen systems, three spreadsheets named final, and somebody's inbox. That's not a reason to stop. It's a reason to know what you're actually working with.

Question three: who owns this on day ninety-one? Not the pilot. Not the demo. The Tuesday after the excitement wears off. If the answer is 'the innovation team,' you don't have an owner, you have a museum.

Here's the thing. None of these are technology questions. They're leadership questions, and that's the point. If you can answer all three, you're ready, and the technology part is honestly the easy part. If you can't, you're not buying AI. You're buying a demo."

## CLIP 4 of 5: B3: "Culture Eats Your AI Strategy"

**Length: 60-90s (~200 words) · Evergreen.**

**HOOK:**
"I've never seen an AI project fail because of the model. I've seen plenty fail because of the culture."

**BODY:**
"The technology works. That's not the risky part anymore. What kills these projects is everything around the technology.

It's the VP who quietly sees the tool as a threat and slow-walks it. It's the team that got punished the last time an experiment didn't pan out, so now nobody experiments. It's the rollout announced so clumsy that half the company thinks it's a layoff with better marketing.

I've watched companies with average models get real results because their people were allowed to try things in the open. And I've watched companies buy the best technology money can get and bury it in a committee.

Culture isn't the soft stuff. It's the operating system the technology runs on. Fix that first and a mediocre model still lands. Skip it, and the best model in the world dies in a conference room with a really nice slide deck."

## CLIP 5 of 5: A1: "Shadow AI Is Already in Your Building"

**Length: 60s (~160 words) · Timely: ties to Episode 17 release week.**

**HOOK:**
"Your employees are already using AI at work. The only question is whether you know it."

**BODY:**
"Every survey says the same thing, and it's a majority. Your people are pasting work into tools you haven't approved, on accounts you can't see.

And look, most of them aren't being sneaky. They're being helpful. They found something that makes the job faster and they're using it, because that's what you hired them to do.

The risk isn't that your people use AI. The risk is that you can't see it. You can't protect data you don't know is leaving, and you can't learn from wins you don't know are happening. Banning it doesn't fix that. It just moves it to a personal phone.

The companies getting this right make it visible. Sanctioned tools, clear lanes, actual training. Your people already voted. You just haven't counted the ballots yet."
