# One Texas 2026: An Action Agenda

**Prepared by:** Quinn (Strategist) | **Date:** 2026-09-17
**Status:** Working draft. Written to be thought with, argued against, and marked up. Not a polished external deliverable.
**Method:** Rumelt's strategy kernel, applied without mercy: diagnosis, crux, guiding policy, coherent actions, sources of power, bad-strategy self-scan, create-destroy.
**Data grounding:** All figures from the live pull of 2026-09-17 (revenue, bookings, pipeline, co-sell, new logos). Derived estimates and measurement gaps are labeled as such. Nothing here is rounded-tripped through stale files.

## What This Is Not

This is not a goals list. The 2026 One Texas targets (2 large-account increases and 4 medium per enterprise, 3 anchor accounts per enterprise, $15M partner co-sell, 10 C-suite connections per enterprise, 10 speaking engagements, EDP in H2, Executive Sales rollout) are the destination. A goals list restates the destination in nine rows and calls it a plan. This document answers the question the targets cannot: what is actually in the way, and what are we going to do about it?

## Diagnosis

**One Texas is two enterprises wearing one name, and the name is hiding the problem.**

Dallas is healthy. Revenue is +4% versus target and +11% versus prior year. The 90-day forecast sits at 101%. Bookings are at 95.2% of the YTD target with $12.7M left on a $40M annual number, and the weighted 90-day pipeline ($13M) already covers the remainder. Dallas needs holding, not fixing.

South Texas is not healthy, on either axis that matters. Revenue is **-21% versus target and -21% versus prior year**. The 90-day forecast is 74%. YTD revenue is roughly $21.5M against roughly $27.2M prior year, so South Texas is running approximately **$5.7M behind last year** *(derived estimate, not an exact figure)*. Bookings are at 88.4% of the YTD pace with $16.9M left on a $46M annual number, and the weighted 90-day pipeline (~$6M) covers barely a third of what the remaining year requires. Dallas carries 90 opportunities at $43.15M of pipeline; South Texas carries 74 at $24.11M. Same company, same brand, same offerings. One enterprise has 1.8x the pipeline of the other and converts at forecast; the other does not.

The growth engine designed to close exactly that gap has stalled. The partner co-sell motion sits at **$6,527,830 against a $15M target, a 56.5% gap ($8,472,170), with a deadline that was Q2 2026 and is therefore already in the past.** Worse than the number: the composition. Only $1.9M of the $6.5M is live pipeline. $4.6M is already won, and the won total has not moved since 2026-09-07. The motion has stopped producing. Confluent, kicked off in Q1, nine months in, holds $785,600 of pipeline. Microsoft holds $373,950. Solace, active since late July with joint targets mapped, holds no co-sell dollars at all. To reach $15M by year-end, South Texas and Dallas together would need to win the existing $1.9M and then generate and win roughly $8.5M net new co-sell in about fifteen weeks. That is not a stretch goal. It is a dead target that nobody has declared dead.

And here is the pattern that makes this a diagnosis rather than a data dump: **the organization's response to the stalled engine has been to build instrumentation around it, not to restart it.** A weekly automated pipeline pull (data collection, no decisions). A monthly sales update (regional, not play-level). A cadence design doc from July 30, still unsigned. A Q3 rocks draft, unsigned for roughly seven weeks while one of its rocks was already past deadline. The measurement layer hums; the number is frozen. Cadence has become a substitute for the hard thing, which is forcing a small number of joint pursuits all the way to won.

Two final facts sharpen the picture. First, the scorecard cannot see the primary 2026 growth mechanism: large-account ($2M+) and medium-account ($750K+) increases are **not tracked in any live source**. That is a measurement gap, not a zero, and it means the group is flying its most important growth goals blind. Second, the rollup lies. "One Texas: 14 new logos, 4 anchors, target met" is true in aggregate and false where it matters: read per enterprise, South Texas is at 1 anchor of 3. The blended number makes the group feel manageable while its growth engine sits frozen in the half of the business that needs it.

**Diagnosis in one sentence:** One Texas's 2026 design routes new growth through partner co-sell into a pipeline-thin South Texas, that engine has stopped converting (relationships aligned, cadence running, joint plays not completing, a $15M target already past deadline and undeclared dead), and the leadership system's habit of building measurement and drafts instead of forcing plays and decisions lets the stall persist.

*Test of the diagnosis: if it is right, we expect to see warm partner relationships with thin attributed pipeline, cadence artifacts without play completions, a dead target still on the books, and aggregate numbers that look better than their worst component. All four are observed. If it were wrong, we would expect co-sell pipeline to be fat but converting poorly, or South Texas pipeline to be deep but stale. Neither is the case: co-sell pipeline is thin ($1.9M), and South Texas pipeline is genuinely shallow relative to its remaining need.*

## The Crux

**The crux is the frozen middle of the co-sell chain: converting an aligned partner relationship into a completed joint account play, end to end, for the first time.**

Not "improving partnerships." Not "tracking co-sell better." Not "growing South Texas" (that is the goal, not the hard part). The hard part is the middle of the chain: partner aligned at the top, account target named at the bottom, and nothing moving through the middle where a joint pursuit gets scoped, sponsored, executed, and won. Improving has proven it can open partner relationships (four live). It has proven it can win co-sell dollars at small scale ($4.6M cumulative, mostly historical). It has not yet proven it can take one named joint play from "we're aligned" to "signed," on a date, with both sides' sellers actually working the same account.

**Defense of the crux, against the alternatives:**

- *Why not "the South Texas revenue shortfall" as the crux?* Because that is a symptom, and partly a statement of dissatisfaction, not the hardest addressable thing. South Texas's direct motion is selling at 88.4% of pace with 74 open opportunities. The shortfall is a pipeline-depth and conversion problem, and the cheapest available pipeline generation for Houston and Austin runs through partners: Solace is Austin-based with a Houston-heavy joint-target list, Confluent has a live referral thread into a nine-year Dallas account. Solve the crux and South Texas gets its missing pipeline from the only motion that does not require hiring, reassigning, or hoping the direct motion suddenly deepens. Fail the crux and no amount of Dallas-style execution discipline closes the gap, because there is nothing to execute against.
- *Why not "the decision vacuum" (seven weeks of unsigned rocks) as the crux?* It is real and it is enabling, but it is a leadership habit, not a market obstacle. It becomes fatal only in combination with the stalled engine. The strategy below attacks it directly (the first action kills the dead rock and signs the quarter), but fixing decision hygiene alone, with the co-sell chain still frozen, produces a well-governed stall.
- *Why not "the measurement gap" as the crux?* Because you can close the measurement gap in a month with a scorecard change, and you would still be blind in a different way: you would be able to see a growth mechanism that still is not working. Instrumentation is necessary, cheap, and not the hard part. (The fact that it has sat untracked all year is itself evidence for the diagnosis above.)

**The crux test, applied:** if we solve everything else, the cadence, the scorecard, the rocks, the speaking calendar, and even South Texas's direct conversion rate, but no joint play completes, does the strategy work? No. Rock 4 misses by millions, South Texas enters 2027 pipeline-thin, and the 3-year vision (eight accounts at $5M+, $115M to $150M in Texas revenue) loses its only scalable engine. If we cannot do the crux, we should not pretend the climb is on.

## Guiding Policy

**Plays, not partnerships. South Texas is the theater; Dallas holds. Report per enterprise, never blended.**

The policy in plain terms: all remaining 2026 growth effort concentrates on a handful of named joint account plays, taken end to end to won, aimed at the South Texas coverage gap, with the first play led personally by David. Everything else is maintenance or truth-telling.

**What this policy rules out (a policy that eliminates nothing is a slogan):**

1. **No new partner relationships.** SpaceXAI stays exactly what it is: light-touch, relationship-driven tracking. No new partner onboarding, no new "strategic partnership" announcements, until the three existing motions (Solace, Confluent, Microsoft) have each completed at least one joint play. Opening a fourth door while three stand open and unused is cadence theater.
2. **No new cadence or meeting load beyond the July 30 proposal, run exactly as written.** The bi-weekly Solace sync, the monthly 30-minute roll-up, the quarterly rotation. Nothing added, nothing folded in, no standing SpaceXAI sync. The agenda of the existing cadence changes (to play milestones, below), but the structure does not grow.
3. **No relabeling.** The $15M co-sell number will not be reached by reclassifying existing direct deals as co-sell, widening the definition, or crediting partner-adjacent revenue that partners did not help win. If the target gets re-baselined, it gets re-baselined honestly, in writing, with a mechanism attached.
4. **No net-new growth initiatives in Dallas.** Dallas's job for the rest of 2026 is to hold a 101% forecast and finish $12.7M. Its pipeline covers it. New Dallas initiatives compete for the same senior selling attention the crux needs.
5. **No blended reporting.** "One Texas" as a reporting unit is retired for the rest of 2026. Every metric, every review, every rock grade is stated per enterprise. If a number only looks good when Dallas and South Texas are averaged, it is not good.
6. **No undefined expansion of David's calendar.** Thought leadership is ahead of plan (Forbes shipped, daily blog running, speaking on pace). It sustains at current cadence. The culture-of-revenue mandate is served this quarter by David visibly leading a play to won, not by adding appearances.

**Why this policy has power behind it:** it aims the group's one true asymmetry (David's personal senior threads into Solace, Confluent, Microsoft, and SpaceXAI, plus nine-year owned account positions like CBRE) at the one target that can actually be moved in fifteen weeks (named accounts where a door is already open, e.g., CBRE via a live inbound Confluent referral). A competitor could copy the words "joint plays." They cannot copy the doors.

## Coherent Actions

Six actions, one spine. They are ordered, and they reinforce each other; remove any one and the others weaken (the arrows are drawn after the list).

**1. Declare the dead rock. Sign the quarter.** *(Owner: David. This week.)*
Rock 4's Q2 2026 deadline has passed with a 56.5% gap. An unaddressed dead target is the clearest possible evidence of the decision vacuum, and it quietly teaches the group that deadlines are decorative. Within two weeks: either re-commit to a co-sell number with a mechanism attached (the play portfolio, below) or reset the number honestly. In the same motion, sign the Q3 rocks. Cut the draft from four rocks to three: (a) First joint plays won, (b) South Texas revenue recovery, (c) Scorecard truth (per-enterprise reporting plus the $2M+/$750K+ instrumentation). The unsigned draft dies at seven weeks old; the quarter starts late but real.

**2. Build the play portfolio: six or fewer named joint plays, one page each.** *(Owner: David with enterprise leads (Don, Diana) and partner owners (Nada, Lowell, Ehren). First draft by Oct 3.)*
Convert the co-sell motion from relationship tracking into a portfolio of concrete plays. Each play is one page: account, partner, entry door, what "won" means, named Improving owner, named partner counterpart, and a next milestone with a date inside 30 days. The first three, chosen for doors already open:
- **CBRE via Confluent.** A nine-year relationship, an active PSA since 2017, and a live inbound referral (Rob Ogbah naming CBRE his key Confluent Cloud customer). This is the nearest thing to an already-won joint play sitting idle. It is play #1.
- **A Solace play into a Houston-area joint target** (from the mapped list: CBRE/AA overlap reconciled first, then Texas Stock Exchange, Caterpillar, Entergy, PepsiCo, Toyota). One account, not the list. The list is a portfolio to choose from, not a workload.
- **One Microsoft SME&C play**, from the existing relationship, chosen by whoever owns it, with the same one-page treatment.
A play without a dated next milestone is not in the portfolio. Six is a ceiling, not a goal; three well-run plays beat six aspirational ones.

**3. Run the July 30 cadence exactly as proposed, with one change of agenda.** *(Owner: David + Chase. Starts within two weeks.)*
The cadence design doc (bi-weekly Solace sync, monthly 30-minute partner-and-pursuit roll-up, quarterly named-account rotation timed to rock review) is signed and run as written. The one change: the roll-up reviews **play-milestone movement**, not data. The question each month is "which plays advanced, which stalled, what does the stalled one need," not "what does the pull say." The automated weekly pull keeps feeding it; nobody re-pulls numbers in the meeting. The cadence is the delivery mechanism for action 2; without it the plays have no heartbeat, and without the plays the cadence is a book club.

**4. Decompose the South Texas gap before aiming all the firepower.** *(Owner: Chase, with the South Texas enterprise lead. Two weeks.)*
The strategy bets that South Texas's miss is a demand-and-pipeline problem that partner plays can address. Test that bet fast. Two analyses by Oct 1:
- **Roll-off decomposition:** which accounts delivered revenue last year that is not repeating this year, and how much of the ~$5.7M YoY gap *(derived estimate)* is roll-off versus new-business shortfall versus delivery-start lag on already-booked work. Note that bookings pace (88.4%) is materially better than revenue pace (-21%), which means part of the gap may live in delivery timing, not sales. If sold-but-undelivered work is the fastest lever, activating it is cheaper than any new pipeline and belongs in the play portfolio's first month.
- **Coverage comparison:** pipeline per seller and win rate, South Texas versus Dallas, to confirm the deficit is depth of pipeline rather than quality of pursuit (74 opportunities at $24.11M against Dallas's 90 at $43.15M is suggestive but not yet causal).
Then aim the play portfolio where the decomposition says the money actually is, which the policy predicts is Houston/Austin coverage.

**5. Per-enterprise scorecard truth, and instrument the blind goals.** *(Owner: David with the scorecard/ops owner. Per-enterprise split immediate; $2M+/$750K+ instrumentation live by Oct 31.)*
Every One Texas review from this week forward reports Dallas and South Texas separately; the "14 logos, 4 anchors, target met" framing is retired in favor of "Dallas 11/3, South Texas 3/1 of 3." Separately: large-account ($2M+) and medium-account ($750K+) increases get instrumented in the scorecard by October 31. Until then they are labeled what they are, a measurement gap. The group cannot manage its primary 2026 growth mechanism if the mechanism is invisible, and it cannot re-baseline Rock 4 honestly if the scoreboard flatters.

**6. David leads play #1 personally, time-boxed, then hands off the pattern.** *(Owner: David. CBRE/Confluent play driven to its next milestone by Nov 30.)*
The stated 2026 responsibility is to be the epitome of GTM strategy and to initiate a relentless culture of revenue. The way to do both in one motion is to personally take the first joint play to its next milestone (not necessarily to won; to its next milestone, visibly, on a date), then extract the pattern and hand it to the enterprise and partner owners for plays two through six. Leading the first one is how the culture mandate gets content; describing it is how it becomes a slogan. The time-box matters: if play #1 consumes David's calendar beyond roughly a quarter of it, the rest of the group's quarter starves, and the handoff by Nov 30 is the discipline that prevents that.

**The coherence arrows:** Action 1 creates the forcing function and frees attention from a dead number. Action 2 is the substance the freed attention goes to. Action 3 is the heartbeat that moves action 2's plays. Action 4 aims the portfolio at the theater where the diagnosis says the money is, and catches the strategy's biggest assumption early (see Create-Destroy). Action 5 makes progress on 2 and 4 visible and keeps 1's re-baseline honest. Action 6 concentrates the one decider on the crux and seeds the culture mandate with a real artifact. Take away 3 and the plays stall silently; take away 5 and the group can't tell stall from progress; take away 1 and the plays compete with a corpse; take away 6 and the plays get delegated below the level where partners commit. This is a system, not a to-do list.

## Sources of Power

Where the asymmetry lives, and why this is not an even bet:

- **Advantage (personal, senior relationship threads).** David holds direct, warm, senior-level threads into Solace (Austin Ledesma), Confluent (Nick Larson, Dante, Rob Ogbah), Microsoft SME&C (John Yurewicz), and SpaceXAI (Cole Estrate). A competitor starting a Texas co-sell motion begins with business development departments and cold outreach. Improving begins with lunches already eaten. This is the sun in the magnifying glass.
- **Leverage (owned account positions).** CBRE is the sharpest case: nine-year relationship, active PSA, named CRM owner (David), mapped leadership org, phased entry plan already written, and a live inbound partner referral. A joint play where both sides already have an open door is the pivot point where a small, concentrated effort produces an outsized, and demonstration-grade, result.
- **Chain-link (the honest reading of Rock 4).** The $15M is a chain output: relationship, alignment, play, pursuit, win. You cannot push a chain output; you can only fix the weakest link, which the diagnosis identifies as play completion. This also explains the stalled won-total: incentives and attention aimed at the number (tracking, pulling, grading) do nothing for the link that produces it.
- **Focus.** Power (the threads and doors above) concentrated through the lens (six or fewer named plays, one theater) onto a target that can be affected (accounts with doors already open, in fifteen weeks). Remove any leg of that triad and the strategy is either dissipated or aimed at something impervious.
- **Proximate objectives.** "First joint play to its next milestone by Nov 30" is feasible with known capabilities and named people. "$15M co-sell by Q2 2026" was not re-examined when it died, which is exactly why it taught the group nothing. Proximate objectives create momentum and learning; dead ones create cynicism.

What Improving does *not* have as a source of power here: a repeatable co-sell machine. That is not a flaw in the strategy; that is what the strategy builds, and the 3-year vision depends on it existing by 2027.

## Bad Strategy Check (self-scan of this draft)

- **Fluff?** Two candidates scanned: "culture of revenue" and "relentless." Both appear only as David's stated mandates, and the draft explicitly refuses to use them as mechanisms; the mechanism given for the culture mandate is action 6, a personally led play with a handoff. Verdict: not fluff, but watch it in any external version of this document.
- **Failure to face the challenge?** The draft names the dead target, the frozen won-total, the seven-week unsigned rocks, and the lying rollup. It faces the hardest fact (the engine stopped producing) in the first screen. Verdict: faced.
- **Goals as strategy?** The $15M, the anchors, and the revenue targets appear as context, never as the approach. The approach is "plays, not partnerships," which describes a mechanism, not a destination. Verdict: clean.
- **Bad strategic objectives (laundry list)?** Six actions, but with a declared spine, an explicit order, and coherence arrows. The priority is unambiguous: the play portfolio is the strategy; everything else serves it. Verdict: a system, not a list. The honest residual risk is that six actions is still a lot for a leader who is also running two enterprises; that is what the "off the table" list is for.
- **The hedging trap?** The draft does not hedge between "fix South Texas directly" and "fix it through partners." It names the bet (partner-led pipeline into South Texas) and names the two-week test that would falsify it. Verdict: committed.

## Create-Destroy

**Create (steel-man):** The two largest numeric gaps in the 2026 picture, South Texas's ~$5.7M YoY revenue shortfall *(derived)* with only ~$6M of weighted pipeline behind $16.9M of remaining bookings, and Rock 4's $8.47M co-sell gap with a deadline already past, are not two problems. They are one problem seen from two angles: the partner motion that was supposed to be the pipeline engine for exactly the Houston/Austin market where the coverage is thinnest has stopped converting. The strategy unifies them through a single mechanism (named joint plays), aims the group's genuine asymmetry (personal senior partner threads plus owned account doors) at it, and sets a falsifiable 90-day signal. That is a real kernel: a diagnosis that simplifies, a policy that rules out five classes of action, and actions that form a chain.

**Destroy (attack it):**

- **The load-bearing assumption:** that South Texas's miss is a demand-and-pipeline problem, fixable with partner-generated pipeline. The evidence cuts both ways: pipeline depth is clearly deficient ($24.11M against Dallas's $43.15M), but bookings pace (88.4%) is far better than revenue pace (-21%). If the dominant driver turns out to be roll-off of prior-year deliveries or slow delivery starts on already-booked work, then partner plays will win deals and the revenue line will still miss, and this strategy collapses into "we won some joint deals and still missed the year." **This is what gets tested first (action 4, by Oct 1), and if the decomposition says delivery timing dominates, the crux shifts: the first plays become activation plays on sold-but-undelivered South Texas work, and the co-sell engine becomes the second quarter's job rather than the first's.**
- **The Solace bet:** the strategy leans on a partner relationship that is seven weeks old and has produced zero co-sell dollars to date. Confluent, not Solace, is the only partner with demonstrated (if small) co-sell production. That is why play #1 is CBRE/Confluent, the proven-ish partner, and the Solace play is deliberately a single account, not the mapped list.
- **The decider's calendar:** David is accountable for the whole group, and action 6 puts him personally on play #1. If the quarter's reality is firefighting, the play starves and the strategy's most visible action becomes its most visible failure. The Nov 30 handoff is the guardrail, not a nicety.
- **The skeptical board member's one-liner:** "You are re-baselining a target downward and calling it strategy." Answer, and it must be made out loud when action 1 happens: the strategy is not the lower number. The strategy is the first completed joint plays; the number is re-baselined because pretending at $15M produces relabeling, and relabeling is what action 3's "no relabeling" rule exists to prevent.

**Rebuild:** What survived the attack is the spine (plays as the unit of work, per-enterprise truth, the dead rock declared). What was adjusted by the attack: action 4 was upgraded from a coverage comparison to a roll-off decomposition that can falsify the diagnosis, and the Solace exposure was capped at one account. Both adjustments are already in the draft above.

## The 2-Minute Action Agenda

**Our challenge:** The co-sell engine meant to generate pipeline for a South Texas enterprise that is $5.7M behind last year *(derived)* has stopped converting: $6.5M against $15M, deadline already past, won total frozen since September 7, while the leadership system builds cadence and leaves drafts unsigned rather than forcing joint plays to completion.

**Our approach:** Plays, not partnerships. South Texas is the theater; Dallas holds. Report per enterprise, never blended. All remaining 2026 growth effort concentrates on six or fewer named joint plays taken end to end to won, beginning with doors that are already open, and the first one is led personally by David.

**First three moves:**
1. *This week:* Declare Rock 4's $15M/Q2 target dead and re-baseline it with a mechanism or an honest number; sign the Q3 rocks (three, not four). *(David)*
2. *By Oct 3:* Build the play portfolio, one page per play, starting with CBRE/Confluent (the live referral), one Solace/Houston account, one Microsoft play. Named owners, dated milestones inside 30 days. *(David, Don, Diana, Nada, Lowell, Ehren)*
3. *By Oct 1, in parallel:* Decompose South Texas's gap (roll-off vs. new-business shortfall vs. delivery lag; coverage vs. Dallas) so the plays are aimed where the money actually is. *(Chase)*

**Off the table:** New partner relationships (SpaceXAI stays light-touch). Any new meeting or cadence beyond the July 30 design, run as written. Relabeling direct deals as co-sell to save the number. Net-new Dallas growth initiatives. Blended One Texas reporting. Added thought-leadership load.

**We'll know it's working when, in 90 days (mid-December):** the first joint play has hit a dated milestone (CBRE/Confluent by Nov 30) and the handoff pattern is written; co-sell won-dollars have moved off the September 7 freeze, with $2M+ of net new won co-sell as the strong signal; the South Texas 90-day forecast has climbed from 74% toward the high 80s; the Q3 rocks were signed within two weeks of this document (if they weren't, the decision vacuum is intact and no other signal matters); and the scorecard shows $2M+/$750K+ account-increase tracking, live by Oct 31.

**We'll know it's wrong when:** the October decomposition shows the South Texas gap is dominated by delivery timing or roll-off rather than demand, in which case the first plays pivot to activation of sold-but-undelivered work, and the co-sell build-out moves to the first quarter of 2027. That is the strategy being a hypothesis, not a plan. Find out fast, adjust, do not deny.
