# Cinemark — Partner Review Brief

**Account Owner:** Hernan Berroja Albiz (South America / Argentina team) · **Region:** South America (LATAM-delivered, US-headquartered client) · **Relationship since:** December 2023

---

## Account Snapshot

Cinemark Holdings, Inc. is a ~$3B, 29,000+ employee movie theater chain headquartered in Plano, TX, operating 497 theaters and 5,653 screens across the US and Latin America — the largest chain in Brazil at 30% market share. Despite the Dallas-Fort Worth HQ, the working relationship runs almost entirely through Cinemark's LATAM/international arm, not the US domestic organization. That's the single most important structural fact for this review: Improving is a trusted vendor to one part of Cinemark and largely unknown to the rest.

Improving delivers Backend, Frontend, DevOps, QA, Data Engineering, and BI/Analytics (Power BI) work with a 15-person, 100% LATAM-based team (average bill rate $50/hr, zero US/CAN or ASIA staff on the account). All delivery runs out of Improving's Buenos Aires office, which sits two blocks from Cinemark's Argentina office — a real proximity advantage the team has leaned on for cadence and trust-building.

## What We're Actually Doing There

- **Platform migration**: Migrating legacy applications to a modern Node/AWS/Serverless/React stack — this is the core, steady-state delivery work and the team's strongest area of client satisfaction.
- **Data & Analytics**: Active data engineering and BI work, described internally as "an area of potential growth" with room to expand beyond current scope.
- **Global delivery**: Cinemark is explicitly cited in Improving's own internal enablement material (the 2026 One Texas SKO deck) as the proof point for LATAM-based Azure data delivery — it's being used to sell other Texas accounts on blended global delivery models.

Two strategic initiatives are stalled: standing up an AI/ML practice on the account (status: Behind/Lagging all year) and participating in Cinemark's exploration of migrating their EDW to Microsoft Fabric (status: Lagging since January, no real movement).

## Competitive Landscape

Cinemark co-staffs its LATAM platform project with **MBLabs** (Brazil) and **Sundevs** (Colombia) — these function more as delivery partners on the same project than pure competitors. **Quisitive**, a Dallas-based Microsoft-focused shop, is the named competitor for domestic/US-side work — worth noting given Quisitive's own Microsoft alliance positioning overlaps directly with ours.

In November-December 2025, Cinemark ran a formal RFI for Application Development rate cards across three experience bands (3-5, 5-8, 8-11 years), run through their sourcing/procurement organization (Ekta Matta, Sr. Commodity Manager). Improving submitted rates. This is a signal Cinemark is formalizing and competitively benchmarking its staff-aug pricing — a procurement-driven pressure point on margin, and no confirmed outcome is on record.

## Partnership / Microsoft Angle

This is the section most relevant to today's review. Cinemark runs Azure as its cloud platform with high satisfaction, and the account is Microsoft-partnered — but the account plan shows **zero deals currently registered** with Microsoft on this account. That's a real gap: there's an active, healthy Azure relationship generating no tracked co-sell motion.

The one concrete Microsoft co-sell touchpoint on record is a **VMware Migration referral**, registered through Microsoft's co-sell pipeline in early 2025 (David O'Hara as the Improving-side contact, working with Microsoft co-sell rep v-ssimara@microsoft.com). There's no confirmed funding (MDF/ECIF) drawn against the account, and no active deal registration as of the current 2026 account plan.

The stalled Fabric migration conversation is the nearest-term co-sell opportunity: Cinemark has been actively exploring moving their EDW to Microsoft Fabric, Improving has a seat at that table via the existing Azure data work, but the account plan has this marked "Lagging" since January with no resumption yet.

## Bottom Line for the Review

Strong, growing relationship on paper (revenue nearly doubled in 2025) that has stalled hard in 2026 on both revenue pace and margin, sitting entirely inside Cinemark's LATAM organization with limited penetration into the US side where the actual economic buyer sits. The Microsoft angle is underdeveloped relative to the health of the underlying Azure relationship: no active deal registration, no funding drawn, and the one live opportunity (Fabric migration) has gone quiet since Q1. If the ask coming out of this review is "where's the co-sell upside," the honest answer is that it exists (Fabric, plus expanding the Azure data footprint) but nothing is currently in motion to capture it.
