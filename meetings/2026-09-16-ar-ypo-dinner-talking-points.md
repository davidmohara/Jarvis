## Purpose of the Evening

The chapter named its own goal: **"The Bridge Year: Linking Leaders Across Arkansas." Subtitle: "From Conversation to Connection."** Read that literally. They are trying to move members from knowing YPO exists to actually using it. The panel exists so the room hears, from people who have done it, that the value is real and specific.

Your success metric is not applause. It is that two or three people come up to you during dessert and ask a real question about how to use YPO.

**Main message:** The single biggest mistake is paying for the whole platform and using only the monthly meeting.

**Suggested phrasing:** "The biggest mistake I see new members make is treating YPO like a calendar entry. You show up once a month, you like the people, and you never open the rest of the box. **Evidence / texture:** Marilyn named M2M as an example she thinks members don't know. YPO Connect is the same story. The catalog is deep and most members never read it.

## Anticipated Q&A

These are Marilyn's own eight questions. Prepare a real answer to each, one or two sentences, then hand it to the room.

**Q: What's the biggest mistake new YPO members make?**
A: They join for the people and then treat it like a subscription. They never open the 80 percent of the platform they already paid for. The fix is embarrassingly simple: pick one network, or one trip, and book it in your first year.

**Q: What do the most engaged members do differently?**
A: They show up before they need something. The people getting the most out of YPO are the ones volunteering to serve on a committee or host a gathering in year one, not year five. YPO rewards giving before taking, and it rewards it fast.

**Q: What resource inside YPO is dramatically underused?**
A: Four of them, in my experience. M2M, where members do real business with each other. YPO Connect, the member platform. The networks, which are open to you regardless of what your chapter is doing. And Concierge, which most members have never called once. Wendy can walk through all of it, and she should.

**Q: What's something you wish every member knew?**
A: That your chapter membership gives you a seat in every chapter in the world. When you travel for work or for fun, reach out to the chapter where you're going. It changes the whole experience of traveling. I've done it, and it's the single most underused perk we have.

**Q: Where do the most powerful relationships actually happen?**
A: Forum first, then shared travel, then tables like this one. Not the stage. If you want the relationships, get into a forum and stay in it, and say yes to one trip a year.

**Q: How should someone use YPO if they're already successful?**
A: As a board of advisors you didn't have to assemble. Use it for the things that keep you up at night, not the things you already know. And be the person who tells a younger member what you got wrong, because that is where the meaning is.

**Q: What's the best "I can't believe YPO gave me this" story you've heard?**
A: Tell a real one from your own chapter building years. If you go with someone else's story, keep it short and specific. The stories land when they are small, not when they are impressive.

**Q: What should Arkansas members take advantage of this year?**
A: The Bridge Year itself. The chapter is coming to you this year instead of asking you to come to Little Rock. Take that deal. Show up in the town you live in, and bring one person with you. And look at GLC and Edge. If you're going to use one big-ticket piece of your membership this year, use it there.

## Landmines

- **Do not pitch Improving.** Not once. This room is a YPO chapter, and you are here as a YPO leader. If a business conversation starts, let it be started by them, and keep it to listening and connecting. You have a Texas base and can make introductions across the region, and that is a gift, not a funnel.
- **Do not sound like a YPO commercial.** You are staff-adjacent tonight but you are not staff. Wendy is staff. If you sell too hard, the skeptic at the table stops hearing you. Your credibility is in the candid answer, including the honest cost of showing up.
- **Do not compare Dallas to Arkansas.** No "we do it bigger" energy. It is a kickoff dinner and the chapter is smaller and younger. Your Lone Star Gold founding story should be offered as a lesson, never as a benchmark.
- **Handle the Chapter Commitment piece generously.** Marilyn asked for this and she is the one who gets pushback from members. Do not let the room hear you siding with the crowd. Frame the requirements as what makes the network worth having.
- **No politics, no 2026 election talk, no Arkansas-versus-elsewhere commentary.** Nothing good can happen there.
- **Nothing confidential about clients or Improving engagements.** You are on the record in a room full of business leaders, one of whom will know someone who knows someone.
