## 2. Company Overview

**Concentrate AI** is a New York City-based AI infrastructure startup founded in 2025 and publicly launched in June 2026. It raised a **$5M pre-seed round** from **True Ventures** and **RRE Ventures**.

**What they build:** An enterprise LLM gateway — a managed operating layer that gives organizations unified API access to every major model (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI, and others) through a single endpoint. Think of it as the API management and governance plane for AI model usage.

**Core product capabilities:**
- Multi-model routing: intelligently distributes workloads across providers to optimize cost, latency, and uptime
- Automatic failover between providers during outages or performance degradation
- Enterprise security stack included by default: SSO, RBAC, audit logging, sensitive-data guardrails, zero data retention
- Real-time spend visibility across teams with per-team usage controls
- No platform markup — tokens billed at cost; the gateway itself is free

**Business model:** Free for the gateway; premium tiers for larger enterprise support. Revenue likely comes from volume enterprise contracts and support tiers. This is a land-and-expand play targeting engineering teams first, then scaling through organizational spend governance.

**Market position:** Competing in the LLM gateway category alongside LiteLLM, Portkey, and similar tools — but differentiating on enterprise-grade compliance posture (free by default, not bolted on) and managed infrastructure (no self-hosting required). Concentrated squarely on the transition from AI experimentation to production deployment.

**Why this matters now:** They launched publicly this month. Ari is in early go-to-market mode. This meeting is likely part of a broader outreach to enterprise technology partners, advisors, or potential customers.

## 5. Talking Points

**Opening — acknowledge the moment**
Concentrate launched publicly this month with $5M behind it and True Ventures on the cap table. That's a real signal. Lead with genuine recognition of the launch and ask Ari how the first weeks have felt.

**During the demo — listen for the enterprise friction points**
Ari's product solves a real problem: enterprises are running AI experiments in silos, burning token budget without visibility, and accumulating compliance risk they don't know they have. As he demos, listen for the specific objections he hears most — that tells you which enterprise buyers are closest to buying and what they're afraid of.

**The services angle — frame it as what makes Concentrate actually stick**
"We see the same problem from the other side. Our clients are building AI capabilities, and the governance and infrastructure layer is always the hard part — not the model choice. What you're building solves the exact problem our delivery teams run into when we try to scale AI inside enterprise accounts."

**One Texas context**
"I run the Texas region for Improving. We have active relationships with enterprise accounts across Dallas, Houston, Austin — exactly the kind of mid-market and large enterprise companies that are in the AI experimentation-to-production transition right now. That's your ICP."

**The partnership question — ask it directly**
"Where does services fit in your go-to-market? Do you have an implementation or channel partner motion, or is that something you're still figuring out?" This surfaces whether there's a formal path or whether David needs to propose one.

**If they're pre-partner-program**
"We'd be interested in being an early partner — we can bring you into conversations where the governance problem is live. In return, when Concentrate is in an account, we'd want to be the implementation option you point to." Clean, reciprocal, low-friction to agree to.

**Ari's pattern — serial exits through enterprise distribution**
Circulate was acquired by LiveRamp largely because of its ability to plug into enterprise identity infrastructure. Concentrate has the same DNA. Ari understands that enterprise distribution is the real moat — he will respond to a conversation about how Improving accelerates his enterprise reach.

**Watch the clock at 25 minutes**
Reserve 5 minutes for a clear "what's next." Don't let the meeting end without a named next step — whether that's Ari connecting David with their partnerships lead, a follow-up call with the technical co-founder, or a specific account introduction.
