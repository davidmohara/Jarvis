## What You're Actually Assessing

### 1. Partnership Philosophy
Ask him to define a "successful partnership" versus a transactional deal. Then walk him through Improving's landscape: Microsoft Alliance, consulting partners, technology partners.

**Strong signal:** He immediately separates strategic (tier-1) from tactical (tier-2), and talks fluently about co-sell versus referral motion without you prompting him.

**Red flag:** Every partnership sounds the same in his mouth. If he can't tell you the difference between a partner that drives pipeline and one that just signs paper, he hasn't built a real program, he's managed a rolodex.

### 2. Program Building & Governance
How did he structure partnership ops at Trianz? Governance, deal approval, tier management. How did he handle partner conflict or misaligned incentives. What metrics did he actually own.

**Strong signal:** He built a repeatable framework, managed cross-functional stakeholders (sales, delivery, legal), and can rattle off the metrics he was accountable for without hedging.

**Red flag:** Everything was "case by case." Single-threaded relationships. When you ask what he owned on the metrics side, you get vague answers about "revenue influence" instead of a number he was actually held to.

### 3. Scaling & Execution
This seat owns co-sell, strategic account overlap, partner enablement, and global coordination. Has he scaled a program past the size he's currently running? How would he build and train a team?

**Listen for:** Process clarity, real investment in people (not just tools), and specifics on enablement infrastructure like partner portals or deal registration.

**Red flag:** He's good at running what exists but has never built the next size up. That's a real gap for this role. Don't let charisma paper over it.

### 4. Leadership Style & Culture Fit
How does he handle a partner relationship gone sideways. His communication cadence and feedback style. Then tell him plainly about Improving's culture: faith-based, values-driven, relationship-first. Watch his reaction, not just his words.

**Key question:** "Describe your ideal working relationship with Sales leadership."

**Strong signal:** He talks about partnerships and sales as one team with shared quota, not two orgs negotiating turf.

**Red flag:** Any hint of "us versus them" between partner org and sales. Territorial language, defensiveness about past conflicts, or blaming previous sales leadership for partnership failures. This is the single fastest way this hire goes wrong, because you cannot coach out territorial instinct in a VP.

### 5. Microsoft Alliance Fluency
Non-negotiable given 70%+ of your revenue runs through the Microsoft ecosystem (Dynamics, Azure, Teams, M365, Power Platform). Ask directly about his Microsoft partnership experience and what a healthy Microsoft partner program looks like.

**Strong signal:** He understands co-sell rigor, competency programs, and Microsoft's incentive structure without you explaining it to him first.

**Red flag:** He nods along but can't name a specific Microsoft program, incentive, or co-sell mechanic. If you're teaching him this in the interview, he's not ready for the job.

### 6. The Texas Scale-Up Test
Tell him where the region is headed: $72M to $100M-$150M in three years, partner co-sell as a critical lever, strategic accounts like Wendy's and Hexagon in play. Watch whether his eyes light up or whether he starts hedging about "assessing the current state first."

Ambition should show up as specific ideas, not caution.

## The Six Questions That Matter

1. "Walk me through your most successful partnership from the ground up. Structure, first 90 days, how you measured success."
2. "What would you do differently running a partner program for a $72M region with 300 people versus the smaller partnerships you've managed?"
3. "We have strong direct relationships with key accounts like Wendy's, Hexagon, AT&T. How do you position partners as complementary instead of stepping on those relationships?"
4. "Describe the last time you told a partner no. How'd you handle it?"
5. "What's your philosophy on partner enablement? Training, tools, comp, recognition?"
6. "If partnerships are generating 15% of new revenue when you start, what's your year-end target, and how do you get there?"

Question 6 is the one that separates operators from talkers. A real number with real logic behind it beats an impressive-sounding "it depends."
