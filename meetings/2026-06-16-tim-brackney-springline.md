## Talking Points

**1. Springline's growth creates a narrow window for this kind of work.**
At 84% growth and 9+ acquisitions, the next 12–18 months either solidify the platform or create compounding fragmentation. AI enablement is most effective when introduced *during* integration — not after the culture and workflows have calcified across 14 offices. The timing isn't arbitrary.

**2. This is a workforce adoption challenge, not a tooling project.**
What Improving brings isn't a list of AI tools to deploy — it's experience getting 500+ professionals who didn't ask for change to actually change how they work. That's harder than the technology, and it's where most firms stall.

**3. Devlin is the engagement lead — not a hand-off.**
Tim should hear directly from David that Devlin owns delivery. Improving doesn't pitch senior people and then hand off to a junior team. What he's been talking to is what he gets.

**4. Trinity Hunt backed this introduction for a reason.**
Davis Fox doesn't refer vendors casually. If the PE network is routing this to Improving, it's because they've seen what Improving does at portfolio companies or with firms like Springline. That's context worth surfacing briefly — it reframes the conversation from vendor evaluation to trusted referral.

**5. Anthony's voice matters — and so does his timeline.**
The Head of Integration is in this call, which means Tim is getting ready to move. Anthony's job is execution. Ask him directly: what does the integration calendar look like for the next two quarters, and where does AI enablement have to fit? His answer shapes the SOW as much as Tim's priorities do.
