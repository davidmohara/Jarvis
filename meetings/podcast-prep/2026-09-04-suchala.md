## Episode Topic

**"Banking on AI, A Chief Banking Officer's View from Inside the Platform Build"**

First United Bank & Trust has been building a real AI and data platform with Improving over multiple engagements: Azure AI Landing Zone, Fabric Landing Zone, AI Data Loss Prevention, a Deep Learning Program, and now a team-extension engagement embedding Improving engineers directly on FUB's AI platform team. Ryan, as Chief Banking Officer, sits above the business outcomes that platform serves — digital mortgage, unified portal, growth strategy — while the engineering itself runs through FUB's platform team.

Core themes:
- Sequencing infrastructure investment against real banking use cases
- Risk, compliance, and guardrails (AI DLP) as a first-class investment, not an afterthought
- Regulatory posture and examiner engagement in a controlled AI program
- Accountability when embedded outside engineers build inside your platform
- Where AI shows up for customers (digital mortgage, unified portal) vs. where it stays back-office
- Build vs. buy vs. sustained-partner model, and realistic timelines vs. vendor hype

**1. Banking Strategy Meets AI Platform Reality**

You oversee strategy, customer engagement, and growth at First United. Improving has been building FUB's AI platform, the Azure AI Landing Zone, Fabric Landing Zone, AI DLP, a deep learning program, and now a team extension embedded directly on your AI platform team. From where you sit as CBO, what changed in the bank's operations once that infrastructure was actually in place, versus when it was still a project on a roadmap?
*→ Which banking function felt the platform investment first: lending, fraud, customer service, something else?*

A lot of banks talk about AI strategy at the infrastructure layer, landing zones, data platforms, governance, before the business side ever sees a use case ship. How did First United sequence that: build the platform first, or let specific banking problems pull the infrastructure into existence?

**2. Risk, Compliance, and the Guardrails Behind the Platform**

First United invested specifically in an AI Data Loss Prevention layer alongside the platform build. That's not a typical first move for a regional bank. What was the risk conversation that made DLP a priority investment rather than an afterthought?
*→ Who owns that guardrail day to day, compliance, IT, or a shared function?*

Regulators are still catching up to how banks use AI in decisioning and customer-facing workflows. With a formal landing zone and governance layer now in place, has that structure changed how you engage examiners, is it now something you can point to as evidence of a controlled program rather than ad hoc experimentation?

*→ Devil's advocate: You're embedding outside engineers directly on your AI platform team rather than keeping the work fully internal or fully outsourced. As the executive whose operations that platform serves, how do you think about accountability and risk when the people building it aren't all FUB employees?*

**3. Where the Bank Actually Feels It**

Your public-facing modernization work, the unified portal, digital mortgage, sits on top of this AI and data infrastructure. Walk us through the connection: what does the deep learning program or the data platform actually make possible in the digital mortgage experience that wasn't possible before?

If AI-driven infrastructure takes over more of the back-office and decisioning work, what happens to the relationship banking model First United has built its reputation on? Is that a tension you manage, or has the platform work so far stayed away from anything customer-facing?

**4. The Executive View on Build vs. Partner**

First United has now closed multiple rounds of AI and data platform work with Improving over what looks like a sustained relationship rather than a single project. What made you keep coming back to the same partner instead of running each phase as its own vendor selection?
*→ What's the honest answer on build versus buy versus partner for a bank your size doing this level of AI infrastructure work?*

*→ Devil's advocate: There's a lot of vendor noise claiming any bank can stand up an AI platform in weeks. Having actually lived through a real multi-phase build, landing zones, DLP, deep learning, now team extension, what's your honest read on how long this actually takes compared to the marketing?*

**5. Wrap-Up**

If you had one piece of advice for another bank leader about to start this kind of platform investment, infrastructure first, guardrails early, partner selection, what's the one thing you'd tell them not to skip?

> What are a couple of takeaways you'd like the listeners to walk away with?
