## Guest Background

**Jorge Pliego**, Chief Financial Officer, Improving (Dallas)

- CFO since 2022, owning financial strategy plus organic and inorganic growth. Runs a finance and accounting team of about 40. On the record: Improving grew "to roughly $100 million before private equity joined us" (Trinity Hunt Partners, whose $618M continuation fund covering Improving closed Oct 2022). This is the ONLY public revenue anchor; do not cite any other figure on air.
- Thirty years of big-company finance before Improving: P&G, Sara Lee Mexico (built the apparel division), Kellogg (Treasury Manager), Avery Dennison (Director of Finance, South America), Diageo (SVP Audit & Risk Americas, then SVP Finance West Latin America). Then AgileThought in Irving: CFO of the legacy company from 2016, CFO of the public entity from August 2021 after the SPAC merger with LIV Capital, a Mexican SPAC, at the absolute peak of SPAC mania. Frame as "through the SPAC-mania window," never "took multiple companies public." One listing, one window.
- Education: ITAM (Mexico City) finance degree, MBA from European University Barcelona, executive education at MIT. Board member of Unidos Somos Iguales, a Mexico-based disability inclusion nonprofit. Avid golfer (Alice's gift: personalized Titleist Pro V1s).
- Public voice on AI: AI is "a very hungry monster. You feed it data, it gives you output, and then it immediately wants more." His advice: start with data quality and platform modernization, not AI tools. Improving's internal Explorer tool scans client platforms for inconsistent data; at one large financial institution it surfaced about 60 remediation projects to fix before an AI solution would generate real benefits. Improving does not sell Explorer.
- M&A discipline, on the record: "We, for the most part, purchase companies a fraction of Improving's size. Even if we make a very wrong decision, 10 percent of our revenue is the worst of the downside." And: "Even if their numbers are great, if we feel the team we're looking to acquire is not a great fit for Improving, we don't move ahead." Also: "Bringing structure and order to chaos is something I like to do."
- Stated CFO priorities: faster closes, utilization focus, and refinancing to fund aggressive M&A.
- Quoted in the Dallas Business Journal (Aug 28, 2026) on unconventional economic indicators and AI strategies. The piece is paywalled, so his unconventional indicator is unknown. Asking him on air is a genuine fresh moment.

**Why he's on this episode:** This is the CFO-audience episode Kristin proposed and David built: one Improving's own CFO talking to finance leaders about the spend-versus-value math of AI from inside a company that sells AI delivery. Jorge has lived both sides of a repricing (a SPAC listing at the peak of the mania, then PE discipline), which makes him the rare CFO who can talk about what happens when the math arrives. The TXSE segment is a genuine first: he has never spoken publicly about the Texas exchange.

## Episode Topic

**"The CFO's Ledger: AI Spend, Org ROI, and What Comes After the Mania"** (working title, TBD per Janine)

Audience: CFOs and finance leaders. The story is the pitch; we do not pitch. Pain points and stories are the thread that lands with similar roles. The spine is Org ROI, the value AI spend delivers to the organization, not sales ROI (that was Episode 3 with Stephen Johnson). The episode is forward-looking: what comes now and what comes next, using the 2021 mania as pattern recognition for today's AI spend.

Core themes (the five acts, locked by David 2026-11-03):
1. From big-company discipline to PE speed, through the SPAC-mania window. Lesson: markets reward stories until the math arrives.
2. His own house: AI meets his accounting and finance function over the next three years. One win, one refusal.
3. Org ROI: the spend-versus-value math. Payback horizon, avoided cost versus new capability, productivity promise versus headcount reality. The Growth Imperative lives here as a universal tension, never with internal numbers.
4. Y'all Street: the TXSE is live, what convinces a CFO it is durable, and what it means for Texas capital formation.
5. North Texas and the five-year view: the financial build-out of DFW, what is next for the CFO function (agentic close, audit prep), and Jorge's next build.

## Questions

**Act 1: From Big-Company Discipline to PE Speed**

**1. The through-line.** Thirty years at P&G, Kellogg, Sara Lee, Avery Dennison, Diageo. Then a SPAC listing in 2021, then PE-backed Improving. What did big-company discipline teach you that still holds, and what did you have to unlearn the week you arrived at a PE-backed company?
*→ Mechanism: What does discipline actually look like on a Tuesday at Improving versus a Tuesday at Diageo?*

**2. The mania, from inside.** You took a company public through a SPAC merger in August 2021, right at the peak of that window. What did watching that window from the inside teach you about how markets price a story versus how they price the math?
*→ Devil's advocate: Someone listening says the SPAC era was just cheap capital and banker fees, no lessons worth keeping. What are they missing?*

**Act 2: AI in His Own House**

**3. The hungry monster at home.** You have said AI is "a very hungry monster": you feed it data, it gives you output, and it immediately wants more. You run a finance and accounting team of about 40 people. Where has AI actually earned its keep inside your own function?
*→ Mechanism: Pick one win. What did it cost, what did it return, and how long before you knew?*

**4. The refusal.** Every CFO I know has a story about an AI spend they refused to fund. What did you say no to, and how did you know?
*→ Devil's advocate: A board member hears that and says refusals are how you fall behind. What do you tell them?*

**Act 3: Org ROI, the Spend-Versus-Value Math (the spine)**

**5. The math itself.** Talk to the CFOs listening about how you actually score AI spend. Not sales ROI, the organization's ROI: payback horizon, avoided cost versus new capability, the productivity promise versus the headcount reality. How do you run that math?
*→ Mechanism: When a president walks in with an AI investment, what are the first two or three numbers you look at?*

**6. The tension every operator feels.** Every leadership team lives this: the pressure to grow pulls spend forward, and the discipline to prove value pulls it back. How do you hold that tension without becoming the Department of No?
*→ Mechanism: What does that look like in practice? A ritual, a threshold, a conversation you force?*

**7. The curve question.** You watched a mania get repriced in real time. Is enterprise AI spend on the same curve? What would tell you the math has arrived?
*→ Devil's advocate: The counter-argument is that AI is infrastructure, like electricity or the internet, and the repricing never comes. Steel-man that for me: what do they get right?*

**Act 4: Y'all Street**

**8. First words on the Texas exchange.** The TXSE has been live since July. Schwab and Texas Capital are primary-listed, and Cullen/Frost just announced it is moving its primary listing off the NYSE. You have never spoken publicly about any of this. As a CFO who has actually taken a company through a listing decision, what convinces you a new exchange is durable?
*→ Mechanism: Pipeline, liquidity, index inclusion. Which one do you watch first, and what number tells you it is real?*

**9. The capital-formation question.** Does a Texas exchange actually change the cost or the path of capital formation for a company operating here, or is that a decade away?

**Act 5: North Texas and the Five-Year View**

**10. The build-out.** Morgan Stanley is building a billion-dollar tower with up to 4,800 jobs. NYSE opened a Texas headquarters. Fifth Third made Dallas its regional HQ, MUFG consolidated in Las Colinas, and DFW is now the number two financial jobs market in the country. What does North Texas look like in five years, and what breaks first under that growth?

**11. What's next for the CFO function.** Faster closes were on your priority list a year ago. Now everyone talks about the agentic close and AI-driven audit prep. What does the CFO function look like in three years, and what are you personally building toward it?
*→ Mechanism: What does the agentic close actually change for your team of 40? What do they stop doing?*

**Wild card (use anywhere it fits, best in Act 5).** You told the Dallas Business Journal your favorite unconventional economic indicator back in August. The piece is paywalled, so our listeners cannot read it. What is it, and why that one?

**12. Closer.** What are a couple of takeaways you'd like the listeners to walk away with?

## David's Talking Points & Angles

- **Personal experience:** David has lived the Growth Imperative tension from the operator's chair all quarter: growth pulls spend forward, discipline pulls it back. He can open Act 3 with that as a universal leadership experience, no numbers, and hand it to Jorge as the man who first put the phrase on the table at Vegas. His own Jarvis multi-agent build also gives him ground truth on what agentic work actually costs and returns, which keeps Act 2 honest.
- **Improving angle:** The Explorer story is Jorge's own quote and the perfect embodiment of "the story is the pitch": Improving scans a client's platforms, finds the 60 remediation projects standing between them and AI value, and does not even sell the tool. Data quality before AI tools. David can connect that to what Org ROI looks like from the delivery side without ever selling.
- **Contrarian or fresh take:** The CFO is not the brake on AI. The CFO is the only person who can make AI spend survive contact with the math. Companies that pair a growth story with a CFO who can price the story are the ones still standing after the repricing.
- **Story to tell:** David's pattern recognition from the 2021 window, companies rewarded for narrative until the math arrived, applied to today's AI spend. It tees up Q7 as a genuine host-guest exchange rather than an interview question.
- **Key frame:** Markets reward stories until the math arrives. AI spend is a story right now, and the CFO's job is to be ready when the math arrives. Meanwhile Texas is building the venue, the TXSE and the DFW financial build-out, where the next chapter gets priced.
