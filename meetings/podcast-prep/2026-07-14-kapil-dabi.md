## 1. Retail's Agentic Commerce Shift

1. Agentic commerce is quickly becoming one of the biggest conversations in retail. For our audience who's still catching up — what does "agentic commerce" actually mean, and why is it suddenly the topic everyone in retail is talking about?
   a. What's pushing retailers to move now instead of waiting to see how this plays out?

2. From your seat working with top retailers, how are brands rethinking product discovery, personalization, and checkout now that an AI agent can complete a purchase without the customer ever landing on the retailer's own site or app?
   a. What happens to the traditional retail funnel when the "storefront" becomes an AI assistant instead of a website?

## 2. AEO vs. SEO — The New Discovery Battle

3. There's a new acronym retail marketers need to learn: AEO — Agent Engine Optimization — alongside SEO. Break that down for us. What is AEO, and how is it different from optimizing for a traditional search engine?
   a. How does a brand make sure its products actually surface when a customer asks an AI assistant to find something, instead of typing a search query?

4. Is there a monetization model for AEO yet, the way there's paid search today? Where do you see that heading?

## 3. Beyond Retail: Agentic Commerce Goes Cross-Industry

5. Retail is the first mover here, but this extends well past retail — travel is next. Walk us through what full trip-booking inside an AI assistant could look like.
   a. What other industries do you expect to follow retail's lead?

6. This connects to some of the work we've been doing at Improving — proactive, AI-driven trip concierge experiences that anticipate what a traveler needs before they ask. Talk about how that kind of "anticipatory commerce" fits into where agentic commerce is broadly heading.

## 4. Google's Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP)

7. Let's get specific. Google recently launched the Universal Commerce Protocol — UCP — with five founding partners: Walmart, Etsy, Shopify, Wayfair, and Target. What is UCP, and how does it differ from what OpenAI has done with its own commerce protocol?
   a. Why did Google build UCP as an open protocol instead of keeping it proprietary to Google?

8. Walk us through what that experience actually looks like for a shopper — say, searching for a chandelier in Gemini and buying it in three clicks, loyalty account and all.

9. **Wrap-Up:** What's the one thing you want retail leaders listening today to go do differently on Monday morning because of this conversation?

## Suggested Flow — Grounded in Kapil's Actual Perspective (from 7/13 lunch conversation)

*(Not speculation — pulled directly from what Kapil said. Useful for anticipating where he'll take each answer.)*

**On UCP vs. OpenAI's ACP:** Kapil draws a clear contrast — "MCP is the protocol to talk to any system. UCP is Universal Commerce Protocol to build agentic experiences on... UCP was a response to OpenAI's ACP, which only allowed checkout. We made the whole experience open — checkout plus product discovery plus personalization." Expect him to lean hard on "open protocol, not locked to Google" as a talking point.

**On the shopper experience:** He'll likely use the Wayfair chandelier example — "When I look for a chandelier in Gemini, I can purchase it without ever going to Wayfair — three clicks. Or it links your Gmail loyalty account so it says, 'Hey, you bought this before, here's a discount.'"

**On AEO vs. SEO:** Kapil frames this as one of the two big open questions in the space right now, alongside UCP itself — "How can we ensure the Google AI Mode or Gemini app brings our product to the top?" He noted the paid/monetization layer for AEO hasn't launched yet, so this is a good moment to press on timing.

**On travel expansion:** He confirmed UCP is heading to travel next — "think about booking an airline ticket directly from Gemini" — which is a clean bridge to the Travelport tie-in question.
