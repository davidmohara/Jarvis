## What You're Actually Assessing

### 1. The Program-Building Story
Make her walk through how she built the alliances function — day one, what was broken, what she personally did versus what the org did around her.

**Strong signal:** Specific decisions, specific sequencing, specific resistance she had to overcome.

**Red flag:** The story stays at altitude. "We aligned stakeholders and drove enablement" with no concrete first move. If she can't name the actual first three things she did, she was present for it, not driving it.

### 2. Revenue Ownership & Commercial Rigor (the flagged gap)
Her alliances function sits outside sales. Husk's own notes say she didn't reference a specific influenced-ARR figure or pipeline coverage ratio. Push past what she says the number *should* be and ask what number she is *actually* held to today.

**Strong signal:** She's candid that she doesn't own a formal number today, and immediately pivots to exactly what she'd propose for Improving — target percentage, timeline, how she'd get executive sponsorship from the CRO-equivalent to make it stick. Candor plus a real plan beats a number she can't back up.

**Red flag:** She invents specificity in the room — a number that sounds precise but wasn't mentioned in her background and can't survive a follow-up question about how it was measured.

### 3. Team & Leadership at Scale (the other flagged gap)
Two direct reports today, both individual contributors on cloud/data-AI ISV relationships. This role likely inherits a larger, multi-layer team. Ask how she'd assess and potentially restructure a 3-4 person team she inherits on day one, and whether she's ever managed out an underperformer or reset someone she didn't hire.

**Strong signal:** She has an actual framework for the 90-day assessment, distinguishes coaching from replacing, and has a real story — even a small one — about resetting or exiting someone.

**Red flag:** Everything she describes is about hiring net-new, never about inheriting and fixing. That's a real gap for a VP walking into an existing team, not a green field.

### 4. Hyperscaler & Microsoft Fluency
This is her strongest scorecard category and matches the Withrow bar: 70%+ of Improving's revenue runs through Microsoft. Confirm it's not just AWS-flavored language dressed up — she should be equally fluent on Microsoft co-sell mechanics, solution areas, and incentive structure.

**Strong signal:** She can speak to Microsoft's program specifics unprompted, not just "we also work with Microsoft" as an afterthought to the AWS relationship that's clearly her deeper one.

**Red flag:** AWS carries the conversation and Microsoft answers stay generic. If Improving is 70% Microsoft and she's 80% AWS-fluent, that's a real mismatch to name, not paper over.

### 5. Culture, Reporting Line, and Fit
She currently reports into marketing, not sales — ask directly why, and what she thinks the right reporting line is at Improving. Also raise faith-based, values-driven, relationship-first culture directly and watch the reaction, not just the words.

**Key question:** "You report into marketing today. Where should Partnerships sit at Improving, and why?"

**Strong signal:** She has a clear, considered point of view — not just "wherever you want me" — and can defend it.

**Red flag:** No opinion, or an opinion that's purely about her own comp/visibility rather than what drives the business outcome.

### 6. Logistics Reality Check
She's Calgary-based with Canadian-dollar-adjusted comp expectations. Don't let this surface for the first time in an offer conversation. Ask plainly: is she relocating, and does she understand this is a Texas-based, in-market seat.
