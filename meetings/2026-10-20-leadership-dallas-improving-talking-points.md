Bethany brought this group to Improving on purpose. Treat her as the co-pilot. Credit her early, out loud, for building the room. Then work the room like a connector, not a vendor.

### 3. The AI angle that lands with a mid-sized company exec: the shift from chatbots to agents, and the governance gap.

**Main message:** Most companies are still using AI one person, one tool, one task at a time, and the gap between that and where the technology is going is widening fast.

**Suggested phrasing:** "Here's the thing I'd put in front of any leadership team right now. Most companies are using AI the way they used their first computer, one person, one tool, one task. The industry has already moved to agents that work in sequence on their own. The reason most companies are stuck isn't the technology. It's that their leadership team is waiting to understand AI before they engage with it. That wait is the risk."

**Evidence / texture:** Improving's Intelligence Operating System framing: progress from Stage 1 (human does the work, AI assists) to Stage 6 to 8 (human orchestrates autonomous agents). The load-bearing point for an executive: autonomy without governance is chaos. Human plus AI at the middle stages outperforms both fully manual and fully autonomous work.

## What to Emphasize with Executives

- **Breadth plus local.** AI, data, application development, and platform engineering, run from Plano. A global firm that is in the room.
- **The AI adoption gap.** The biggest risk is a leadership team that waits to understand AI before engaging with it. That reframes the conversation from vendor to peer advisor.
- **Culture as competitive advantage.** Relationships over transactions, Conscious Capitalism, trust. It is the differentiator a competitor cannot copy.
- **The people in the room.** Improving's strength is its bench. Don on learning and growth, Diana and Derek on accounts, Kevin on delivery. Introduce, then let them work.

## Natural Conversation Starters

- "What's keeping you up at night right now?" (works with any executive, opens the real conversation)
- "How is your team actually using AI today, not the plan, the reality?" (invites honesty, not a pitch)
- "What stuck with you from your Leadership Dallas year?" (shared experience, warms the room, gives Bethany's group its moment)
- "You're in Dallas. What's your read on where the business community is heading?" (local, peer to peer)
- "What would have to be true for you to move on the AI question this quarter?" (a question that qualifies without selling)

## What NOT to Say

- **No formal Improving capabilities presentation.** No slides, no "let me tell you about our services." This is a breakfast, not a briefing.
- **No internal numbers.** Revenue, pipeline, co-sell targets, and the One Texas strategy are internal. None of it belongs in this room.
- **No hard sell.** These are peers who came to reconnect. Pitch energy kills the room.
- **Do not lead with faith.** Faith is present in how you live, not a sermon opener. Conscious Capitalism is the language for a business room, and only if it comes up naturally.
- **No over-promising on AI.** Do not say Improving can "automate everything" or "do AI for you." The honest, defensible line is the adoption gap, not a guarantee.
- **Don't monopolize.** It's Bethany's event. The more you talk, the less the room connects.
- **No competitor bashing.** It reads as insecurity in a peer room.
- **Don't make it about your resume.** Co-founder and 14 years as president are credibility, not content. Let it do its work in the background.

## If Asked

**Q: So what does Improving actually do?**
A: We're a modern digital services firm. AI, data, application development, and platform engineering. Founded in 2007, about 2,500 people, 21 offices worldwide, and our Texas business runs out of Plano. If it's software, data, or AI, we probably help someone with it.

**Q: How should our company be thinking about AI right now?**
A: I'd start with the honest question, which is whether your leadership team is engaging with AI or waiting to understand it first. Most are waiting, and that's the real risk. The technology has moved from single chatbots to agents that work in sequence on their own. The companies getting this right aren't the ones with the best tools. They're the ones with governance that lets them move without losing control. I'm happy to walk through how we frame it.

**Q: What makes Improving different from the other firms we've talked to?**
A: Two things. One, we're builders, not slide-deck consultants. We ship software, data, and AI every day. Two, the culture. We've built a place where people say the culture is why they stay, and that shows up in the work. That's harder to copy than any offering.

**Q: Are you hiring? How do you find and keep talent?**
A: Always, for the right people. Talent is the whole game in this business. Don McGreal runs our Learning and Growth practice, and he's here this morning. He's the right person to talk to about how we build our people. Let me introduce you.

**Q: Could you help us with [specific problem]?**
A: Maybe. I'd rather hear the real version of the problem before I answer that. Grab a coffee with me or one of the team and tell me what's actually going on. If we're the right fit, great. If we're not, I'll tell you that too.

**Q: What do you personally get out of events like this?**
A: The relationships. That's the part of the business I care most about, and it's the part you can't automate. Dallas has a real community of people building things. I'd rather be in the room with them than reading about them.
