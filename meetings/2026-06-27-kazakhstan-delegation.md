## 3. Kazakhstan AI Context

- **2026 is Kazakhstan's declared "Year of Digitalization and Artificial Intelligence."** President Tokayev made it a national priority. The country created a dedicated Ministry of AI & Digital Development in 2025, elevating AI to deputy prime minister-level governance.
- **National AI Concept runs 2024-2029**, structured around three pillars: infrastructure, human capital, and regulation. A full National AI Strategy is being developed to upgrade it.
- **Kazakhstan enacted its AI law** (No. 230-VIII) in November 2025, in force since January 2026. Risk-based structure similar to the EU AI Act framework, with heightened requirements in public administration, critical infrastructure, finance, healthcare, and security.
- **Infrastructure is moving fast.** In June 2026, Kazakhstan signed a $10 billion agreement with Firebird and NVIDIA to build a Data Center Valley in Ekibastuz (100,000 NVIDIA GPUs planned). They already launched a national supercomputer in 2025 powering KazLLM and AlemLLM, sovereign LLMs in Kazakh and Russian.
- **AI literacy at scale.** The AI-Sana program has reached over 650,000 students. Over 2,500 civil servants trained in AI since early 2025.
- **Digital governance is legitimately advanced.** Kazakhstan ranks 24th globally on the 2024 UN E-Government Development Index, leads CIS countries, and has 92%+ of government services online.

These aren't a country just starting out. They're building fast and looking for partners who can match that pace.

## 7. Protocol Notes

**Diplomatic register:** These are government officials on an official State Department exchange. Formal but not stiff. They have spent three weeks in the U.S. and are experienced at engaging with American professionals by now.

**Interpreters:** Always speak to the principals, not the interpreters. Use clear, direct sentences. Pause every 2-3 sentences. Avoid idioms, sports metaphors, and American pop culture references. No sarcasm.

**Acknowledge the journey:** It is appropriate -- and genuinely warm -- to acknowledge that this is their final stop after three weeks and that it is meaningful to be part of that. Don't overdo it, but don't skip it either.

**First U.S. visit (principals):** Some cultural warmth is appropriate. A brief acknowledgment of how you hope the trip has been goes a long way.

**Kazakh welcome:** Bethany suggested opening with a welcome slide including the Kazakhstan flag and a greeting in Kazakh.

- Written: **Qош келдіңіздер**
- Transliteration: **Qosh keldingizder**
- Pronunciation: "KOSH kell-DING-iz-der"
- Meaning: "Welcome" (plural, formal)

Even an imperfect attempt will land well. Say it, explain it, move on. It signals respect.

**Gift / exchange note:** If business cards are exchanged, accept with both hands or right hand only (not left). A brief moment of genuine interest in the card before pocketing it is appropriate.
