## Improving's Position

Improving is a US-based technology consulting firm — not a staff augmentation provider. The model is Improving consultants on Improving engagements: we sell our people, our culture, and our practices into client accounts. We do not white-label external talent or resell third-party engineers. That distinction matters here and should be established early so neither party wastes time on a misaligned pitch.

That said, there are legitimate partnership shapes worth exploring:

**Referral exchange.** The most natural play. If Techbar encounters a client whose need is primarily advisory, process transformation, or embedded consulting (not headcount), that's an Improving conversation. In the other direction, if Improving encounters a client who needs to rapidly scale a delivery team with Eastern European engineers and Improving's bench isn't the right fit, Techbar could be a referral destination. This works if both firms are honest about where their models end and the other's begins.

**Co-sell into enterprise accounts.** Lower probability but possible. If Techbar has an enterprise relationship where the client wants deeper consulting or architecture capability alongside their dev team, Improving could add value there. The inverse is also true — Improving clients occasionally need more raw engineering throughput than we carry on the bench.

**Sub-contractor or bench access.** Lowest probability given Improving's model. Worth understanding Techbar's quality bar and vetting process if this ever comes up, but do not lead with it.

No prior Improving-Techbar deal history found. David enters this as a clean slate with no commitments and no relationship equity — which is both a constraint and an advantage. He can be fully direct about fit early.

## Talking Points

**1. Let her show her hand first.**
Open with: "Kate, what were you hoping to explore on this call?" Kate initiated this outreach — she has a thesis. Surface it before framing anything from Improving's side. Her answer tells you whether this is a referral pitch, a co-sell play, or a bench inquiry.

**2. Understand who their clients actually are.**
Ask about industries, company sizes, and geographies Techbar serves. This determines whether there is any realistic client overlap. If their typical buyer is a 20-person startup in Spain, the referral loop probably doesn't close. If they're working with mid-market or enterprise accounts in the US or Western Europe, the conversation gets more interesting.

**3. Be direct about the Improving model early.**
"We staff Improving consultants into client engagements — that's the model. We don't generally white-label or resell external talent. So the most natural partnership, if there is one, is probably a referral exchange or a co-sell scenario where both firms bring distinct value to a shared account." Saying this at minute 15 saves both of you from a 30-minute misalignment.

**4. Probe the CHI Software connection.**
"Your background is CHI Software — how does Techbar differentiate from that world?" It shows you did homework and gets her to articulate Techbar's actual identity. It also clarifies whether this is a CHI spinoff, an alumni venture, or a fully independent entity — which affects how you assess their stability and footprint.

**5. If fit exists, get it concrete.**
If the referral or co-sell angle lands, push toward specificity: "Is there a client or an opportunity you have in mind, or are we building a relationship we'd activate later?" The difference matters. A warm lead in the room changes the urgency of everything else.
