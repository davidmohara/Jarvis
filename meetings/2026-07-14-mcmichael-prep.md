## What's Been a Challenge — Named Directly

### 1. Target Account Pursuit — No New Anchors Closed H1

The pipeline is active and the names are right (AT&T, CBRE, Builders First Source, Tenet, Texas Instruments in Dallas; Marriott, Kirby Corp, Expedia, Masimo, Constellation in Houston). But none have crossed the line into anchor status.

The pattern: engagement is happening at the AE level, but the RD role is most effective when it creates top-to-top access and accelerates deals that are stalled or not yet opened. The cadence and coordination between David's relationships and the local sales team's pipeline needs to be tighter in H2.

### 2. Being Sold as Billable — This Needs to Be Said Plainly

There have been situations in H1 where David has been pulled into engagements in a billable capacity. This is a structural problem, not a personal one.

The Regional Director role is defined as a GTM function: it creates access, builds relationships, drives strategy, and accelerates revenue. The moment the RD becomes billable, three things happen simultaneously: (1) the GTM capacity disappears from the hours consumed, (2) it signals to the market and to internal teams that the RD is a delivery resource, and (3) it undercuts the executive presence that makes the RD model work.

This isn't a complaint — it's a design constraint. If the role occasionally requires David to step in on a high-stakes engagement, that should be the exception with explicit mutual agreement, not a default when capacity is tight. The conversation with Scott should establish clarity: what are the guard rails, and who owns the decision when a request to pull David in gets made?

## Regional Approach — Recommendations for Expansion

Scott's ask: your recommendations as Improving evaluates expanding the Regional Approach.

### What's Working (the case for expansion)

1. **The GTM motion is real.** Partner relationships, thought leadership, account acceleration — these are things a Regional Director can do that a President running P&L cannot prioritize. The Confluent partnership, the Microsoft C-suite relationships, and the Systemic Compliance win all have David's fingerprints on them.

2. **Texas is a proof point in progress.** One Texas is behind on revenue but the infrastructure is being built — cadence, partner motion, directed pursuit model. The model needs more runway, not a verdict yet.

3. **The podcast, workshops, and speaking create market presence** that individual enterprises don't generate on their own. That's a regional asset.

### What the Model Needs to Work

1. **Clear role definition — especially around billable.** As noted above. Without this guardrail, the RD capacity gets consumed by delivery and the GTM function hollows out.

2. **A President layer that's truly operating autonomously.** The RD model works when Presidents are running their P&L and the RD is adding above-line value. Where Presidents are still leaning up (resource ops, HR, client management), the RD gets pulled into operational mode. Expansion requires Presidents who are fully equipped and empowered.

3. **Revenue attribution clarity.** Co-sell, partner influence, RD-accelerated deals — these need to show up in reporting. If the RD's contribution is invisible in the numbers, the ROI of the model is unprovable and the expansion case weakens. Recommend building an attribution framework before the next region launch.

4. **A minimum viable market size.** Texas at ~$89M total run rate (Dallas $41.6M, Houston $24.8M, Austin $22.7M per PowerBI) justifies one RD. The question for expansion: what's the revenue floor that makes an RD economically rational? And is the next region already above it, or is the RD investment meant to get it there?

### Recommendation

Expand — but do it with the guardrails the Texas model revealed are necessary. Define the role clearly (especially billable boundaries), ensure the President layer is ready, and build revenue attribution into the reporting framework from day one. Don't replicate the ambiguity.
