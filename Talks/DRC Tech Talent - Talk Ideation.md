# DRC Tech Talent Talk — Ideation

**Context:** Dallas Regional Chamber invited a debrief/talk following Improving's participation in Convergence AI 2026 (March 30-31, Irving Convention Center). Bethany Hilton forwarded the request from Aaliyah Avellaneda (DRC Research & Innovation). Table-talk format — intimate, discussion-based, not a keynote. Audience is DRC members focused on tech talent and workforce.

**Format from prior Convergence AI engagement:** Table talk, ~55 min (12:05–1:15pm). Conversational, not slide-heavy.

---

## Core Tension to Anchor the Talk

> "The software jobs aren't dying — they're mutating. And Dallas needs to know the difference."

The DRC audience cares about talent pipelines, employer readiness, and regional competitiveness. Frame everything through that lens: what does this mean for companies hiring in DFW, and for the people trying to get those jobs?

## Proposed Talk Structure

### 1. Open: What We Saw at Convergence AI

- Executives, technologists, practitioners all in one room wrestling with the same question: *what does AI actually change?*
- Use this as a "we were just in the room" credibility anchor
- The honest answer: nobody really knows yet. But the signals are clear.

---

### 2. The Panic Is Real But Misdiagnosed

**Source:** [CNN Business — "The demise of software engineering jobs has been greatly exaggerated"](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/08/tech/ai-software-developer-jobs) (April 8, 2026)

- Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff said the company **stopped hiring engineers** last year
- **~50% of the US public** believes AI will lead to fewer software jobs in the future (CNN)
- University of Washington CS director emailed 2,000+ undergrads: *"AI is not killing your job options. It's expanding them."*
- **Software developer job listings on Indeed are up 11% annually** — faster than overall postings (Citadel Securities)
- **Bureau of Labor Statistics:** Software developer employment projected to **grow 15% by 2034**
- IBM is **tripling entry-level hiring** in the US, including software developers
- Companies expanding software budgets and increasing engineer headcounts (Bank of America survey)

**Key insight:** AI makes software cheaper to produce → demand for software goes up → demand for engineers goes up. Historical parallel: textile automation drove a 100x increase in cotton cloth consumption, and textile employment *soared* until the 1960s. (James Bessen, Boston University)

---

### 3. But the Job Is Fundamentally Changing

**Source:** [CNN Business](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/08/tech/ai-software-developer-jobs)

- Engineers doing **less routine coding**, more **overseeing AI agents**
- More time on **architecture, design, customer problem-solving**
- IBM's shift: from writing code → working directly with customers and specifying features for AI to build
- Intuit's shift: engineers now focus on complex code and software design, not boilerplate — *"They actually have the time to worry about those things because they don't have to spend endless hours coding boilerplate."* — Alex Balazs, Intuit CTO
- The companies that win will connect **junior AI power users** with **senior employees who understand the industry** (Frank Nagle, MIT Initiative on the Digital Economy)

**Provocative point for the room:** The most dangerous place to be is mid-career, mid-skill, and reluctant to change. Senior enough to be expensive, junior enough not to have locked-in relationships.

---

### 4. The Hiring Process Is Also Broken — In Both Directions

**Source:** Business Insider — *"Why every job description suddenly feels like a CVS receipt"* — Amanda Hoover

**Stats (all cited with sources):**
- Average **job title length** jumped from **2.4 words (2013) to 4 words** last year — *BambooHR analysis of job listings*
- Average **job description character count** up **7.4%** from 2022 to 2026, the four years since ChatGPT went mainstream — *Greenhouse*
- **Number of sections** in a job listing grew **~14%** over same period; **skills sections jumped ~16%** — *Greenhouse*
- **Words per post** grew **14.3%** from 2021 to 2025 — *Indeed*
- Job descriptions increased **17% in character length** from 2016 to 2025, biggest acceleration 2021–2022 — *BambooHR*

**Why this matters for DFW employers:**
- Managers using AI to draft JDs are *"dumping the kitchen sink in there — nice-to-haves, could-haves, and passing thoughts they had in the shower this morning."* — Marc Cenedella, CEO of Ladders
- Result: qualified candidates self-select out
- **Harvard Business School research:** Women are less likely to apply if they don't meet every stated qualification — bloated JDs disproportionately hurt diversity pipelines
- Meanwhile, applicants run résumés through ChatGPT to match keywords — so recruiters can't tell who can actually do the job
- *"More paragraphs doesn't make for a better hire. More bullet points doesn't make for a more on-target fit."* — Cenedella

**The fix:** Write JDs like a scorecard. The earliest known job ad (1752, Virginia newspaper) had two qualifications: *"a sober person, well recommended."*

**Counterexample worth telling:** Jamie Hodari, CEO of Industrious, wrote a job description for his own successor that ditched corporate speak entirely — listed the actual humans the CEO would manage. Called it *"the single most effective recruiting-related thing I've ever done in my career."* Authentic, deeply personalized applications poured in.

---

### 5. What Dallas Employers Should Actually Do

**Employer side:**
- Audit your JDs — if it's longer than a page, it's probably hurting you
- Prioritize skills-based hiring (already a Dallas Thrives / DRC focus)
- Stop treating AI proficiency as an add-on layer — redesign the role around what AI actually changes
- Hire for learning velocity, not credential match. IBM and Intuit are doing this explicitly.

**Talent/pipeline side:**
- DRC Talent Labs 2026 cohort has a dedicated **"AI & Workforce Capacity"** session in November — good signal the region is moving
- UTD Jindal School has an AI Council with senior industry leaders — plug into that
- DFW's fastest-growing jobs in 2026: **AI engineers and AI consultants** (LinkedIn data via Dallas Innovates)

**For tech workers themselves:**
- *"Roll up your sleeves"* — Magdalena Balazinska, UW CS director
- The most valuable engineers use AI all day, every day to make their designs better (Amanda Richardson, CoderPad CEO)
- Junior developers: don't let AI become a crutch that hollows out foundational skills

---

### 6. The Improving Angle

What we're seeing on the ground:
- Clients aren't asking "can you build this?" anymore — they're asking "how do we govern what AI builds?"
- Demand for consultants who can bridge business outcomes and AI capability — not just coders
- Training and coaching is resurging — companies realize their people need to level up, not just their tools
- The firms winning are treating AI adoption as an **organizational change problem**, not a technology procurement problem

## Table Discussion Questions

1. What's your biggest hiring headache right now — finding the people, or knowing what to look for?
2. How is your organization thinking about reskilling existing engineers vs. bringing in new talent?
3. Are your job descriptions working for you, or are you getting résumé noise from AI-optimized applications?
4. What would it take to make Dallas the best place in the country for AI-era tech talent?
